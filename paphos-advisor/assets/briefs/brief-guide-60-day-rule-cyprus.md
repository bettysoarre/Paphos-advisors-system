## Key Points to Cover

**Must include:**
- The two routes to Cyprus tax residency: 183-day standard rule vs. 60-day alternative rule
- The four conditions of the 60-day rule (all four must be met):
  1. Minimum 60 days in Cyprus (days of arrival and departure both count; non-consecutive days are fine)
  2. No more than 183 days in any single other country
  3. A permanent home available in Cyprus throughout the year (owned or rented; must be genuinely available, not nominally held; holiday lets during part of the year may not qualify)
  4. Cyprus employment, directorship, or business activity through 31 December (directorship of a Cyprus company is the most common solution)
- **The 2026 change:** The fifth condition (must not be tax resident anywhere else) was removed from 1 January 2026. Dual tax residency is now permitted. Source this clearly: PwC Direct Tax Update N1/2026; Harneys January 2026.
- What counts as economic activity (directorship, employment contract, active business) vs. what does not (remote work for non-Cyprus employer, passive investment income, owning a company without a directorship)
- Dual residency risk: holding Cyprus tax residency while remaining registered elsewhere — how double tax treaties resolve this; tie-breaker criteria (permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality); why cross-border tax advice is essential before relying on Cyprus non-dom status
- Travel record-keeping: both arrival and departure count; keep records throughout the year (boarding passes, hotel receipts, travel diary); do not try to reconstruct retrospectively
- The step-by-step process: satisfy conditions → keep records → register for TIC → Taxisnet account → file annual return (TD.1) → request Tax Residency Certificate if needed
- Connection to non-dom status: the 60-day rule establishes tax residency; non-dom status is the separate application that delivers the SDC exemption on dividends and interest — the two processes run in sequence

## Structure / Outline

### The 60-day rule: what it is and who it is designed for (~200 words)
The two-route system: 183-day standard vs. 60-day alternative. The 60-day rule exists for internationally mobile individuals and business owners who cannot commit the majority of the year to one country. Cyprus tax residency is the prerequisite for non-dom status. Brief, punchy opening — no lengthy background.

### The four conditions you must meet (~400 words)
All four must be satisfied in the same calendar year. Cover each clearly:

  #### 1. At least 60 days in Cyprus
  Days of arrival and departure both count. Non-consecutive fine. Aim for 65+ if close to the threshold.

  #### 2. No more than 183 days in any single other country
  This limits time in one country — not total days abroad. Multiple countries are fine; 183+ in any one is not.

  #### 3. A permanent home in Cyprus all year
  Owned or rented. Must be genuinely available — not let to holiday tenants during peak season. Stamped 12-month rental agreement is the standard evidence.

  #### 4. Cyprus business activity through 31 December
  Directorship of a Cyprus company is the most common route. Employment contract with a Cyprus employer also qualifies. Remote work for a non-Cyprus employer does not satisfy this condition alone — a Cyprus company directorship is typically needed. Link to company formation article.

### The 2026 change: what dual tax residency means in practice (~300 words)
The fifth condition (must not be tax resident elsewhere) was removed from 1 January 2026. What this means: you can now hold Cyprus tax residency while remaining registered in Germany, the Netherlands, or the UK. What this does not mean: it does not remove home-country tax obligations. Where two countries both claim residency, the double tax treaty determines primary taxing rights. Tie-breaker sequence: permanent home → centre of vital interests → habitual abode → nationality. This is the highest-risk area in the article: cross-border tax advice is essential. Source clearly: PwC + Harneys.

### What qualifies as Cyprus business activity — and what does not (~200 words)
Qualifies: Cyprus company directorship; Cyprus employment contract; active Cyprus business operations. Does not qualify on its own: remote employment for a non-Cyprus employer; freelancing for non-Cyprus clients; passive investment income from Cyprus sources; owning a Cyprus company without a directorship. Practical implication for ICP-SEG-004: if the client does not yet have a Cyprus company, company formation is the prerequisite. Link to Cyprus Company Formation Guide.

### How to establish Cyprus tax residency: step by step (~300 words)
Follow PROC-TAX-PROC-001 steps 1–6:
1. Satisfy all four conditions during the calendar year
2. Keep records throughout the year (boarding passes, hotel receipts, travel diary)
3. Register for a TIC
4. Create a Taxisnet account
5. File annual tax return (TD.1) the following year
6. Request Tax Residency Certificate if needed (for foreign tax authorities or banks)

### The connection to non-dom status (~150 words)
The 60-day rule establishes that you are a Cyprus tax resident. Non-dom status is what you apply for separately — it is what delivers the SDC exemption on dividend and interest income for up to 27 years. Brief explanation of the sequence; link to non-dom article.
