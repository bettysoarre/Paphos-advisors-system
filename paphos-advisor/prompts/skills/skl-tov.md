# SKL-TOV — Tone of Voice

## Trigger

Activate this skill whenever producing, briefing, or reviewing any content written on behalf of Paphos Advisors — website guides, FAQs, blog posts, social posts, emails, CTAs, or any reader-facing copy.

---

## Core Voice — Who We Are

Paphos Advisor sounds like the most knowledgeable local friend you have in Paphos. Not a corporate services firm. Not a travel brochure. A trusted expert who has navigated Cyprus's systems hundreds of times and speaks plainly about what actually happens.

Four qualities define the voice at all times:

| Quality | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Professional yet human** | Authoritative but never stiff — warm without being informal |
| **Direct and honest** | We say what people need to hear, not what sounds nice |
| **Reassuring and calm** | We reduce anxiety around complex processes — we do not add to it |
| **Educational** | We explain the *why* behind every recommendation, not just the what |

---

## Language Rules

### Use plain English first
- Introduce any technical, legal, or Cyprus-specific term immediately followed by a plain-language explanation
- Do not assume the reader knows what KEP offices, title deeds, or MEU1 forms are — name them, then explain them in the same sentence or the next one

### British English throughout
- Organisation, neighbour, licence, colour, recognise, practise (verb) / practice (noun)
- No Americanisms — see SKL-GRD for the full list

### Cyprus- and Paphos-specific references
- Use local references confidently: municipality, Land Registry, CRMD, KEP offices, title deeds, MOT — these establish authority
- Always explain them on first use; never use them as shorthand that assumes local knowledge

### Vocabulary to avoid
- Generic "expat" clichés — do not write as if for a relocation magazine
- "Sun, sea, and sand" — our edge is practical intelligence, not lifestyle marketing
- Corporate buzzwords — "synergy", "leverage", "solution-driven", "holistic approach"
- Vague positivity — "exciting opportunity", "seamless experience", "world-class service"

---

## Sentence Structure

### Rhythm
- Open with a short, punchy sentence that makes the point immediately
- Follow with slightly longer explanatory sentences that provide context
- Close sections with a clear, concise summary or next step — never trail off

### Lists
- Use bullet points for non-sequential items (documents needed, things to know)
- Use numbered lists for sequential processes (application steps, timelines)
- Never use lists as a substitute for explanation — a bullet point that needs three sentences of context should be a paragraph

### Cause and effect
- Structure arguments as: action → consequence or choice → risk/reward
- Make the stakes explicit: "If you do X, Y happens. If you skip X, Z happens instead."

**Example:**
> "Accuracy is key. Cyprus has systems in place that can slow you down for months if a single document is wrong."

---

## Narrative Style

### Point of view
- **"We"** = Paphos Advisor as the expert guide — use for advice, recommendations, and process explanations
- **"I"** = a specific advisor speaking from personal experience — use sparingly, only when a real personal observation adds credibility
- **"You"** = always the reader — direct address throughout; never "the client" or "relocators"

### Perspective
- Write as if the reader already intends to move, invest, or retire in Paphos — not as if they might
- Future-focused: show the next 6–24 months, not just the immediate step
- Solution-oriented: acknowledge that Cyprus can be confusing, then show exactly how we simplify it

### Storytelling
- Short real-world scenarios work well — "We've seen buyers lose entire summers to avoidable delays because nobody checked the title situation at the start."
- Scenarios must be realistic and specific; never invented or embellished
- Purpose of a scenario: show what actually happens when advice is followed or ignored — not to scare, but to inform

---

## Persuasion

### Logic first, emotion second
- Persuade by explaining how the system works and what the smart move is — not by appealing to fear or aspiration alone
- Use concrete specifics where possible: timeframes, typical fee ranges, common pitfalls
- Do not overcomplicate with caveats — one clear recommendation, with the reasoning behind it

### Risk–reward framing
- Show the cost of getting things wrong in Cyprus, then show the benefit of doing it properly once
- Do not scare — do inform: "This is a known risk. Here is how we handle it."
- Never omit a real risk to make the process sound easier than it is

### Reader engagement
- Ask direct, relevant questions that make the reader reflect on their situation — not generic ones
- Good: "Are you about to sign anything in Paphos without a second pair of eyes on it?"
- Not: "Are you thinking of moving to Cyprus?"

## Proprietary Language

Use these Paphos Advisor phrases naturally in content — they establish the brand's distinct positioning:

- **"Paphos-proof planning"** — preparing documentation and decisions to withstand Cyprus's administrative complexity
- **"Relocation health check"** — an initial assessment of a client's readiness
- **"Title deed sanity check"** — a due diligence review of property ownership records
- **"Local pilot"** — the metaphor for Paphos Advisor's role (navigating bureaucracy on behalf of the client)

Do not invent new proprietary phrases without instruction. Do not overuse these — one or two per article maximum.

## Humour and Tone Calibration

### Humour
- Subtle and professional — shows personality without undermining the seriousness of legal or financial matters
- Light, knowing comments about quirks of local processes are appropriate: "Yes, the queue at the office can feel endless — unless you know exactly which window to go to."
- Never mock individuals, cultures, or the reader's situation
- Never use humour in sections covering legal obligations, regulatory deadlines, or financial risk

### Emoticons / emoji
- Avoid in all core website content, guides, FAQs, and process pages
- Acceptable sparingly in informal channels (some email, some social posts) — when in doubt, leave them out

## Content Flow

Every piece of content should follow this logical sequence:

1. **Problem / question** — what the reader is facing or asking
2. **Context in Cyprus/Paphos** — why this is the way it is here (regulatory, cultural, practical)
3. **Options** — what the reader can do
4. **Recommended path** — what Paphos Advisor suggests and why
5. **Next step** — a clear, low-friction action

One main message per piece. Echo it throughout in different words — do not repeat verbatim, but do reinforce.

---

## Calls to Action

CTAs must be specific and low-friction:
- "Book a free 30-minute call" not "Contact us"
- "Send us your situation in three bullet points" not "Get in touch"
- "Request our [specific checklist name]" not "Download our resources"

Never use: "Learn more", "Click here", "Find out more" — these are placeholders, not CTAs.

---

## Anti-Patterns

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Cyprus is a sunshine destination perfect for retirees" | Describe the specific benefit relevant to this reader's situation |
| "Our world-class team of experts" | "We've handled [X type of case] dozens of times — here's what we've learned" |
| "This is a seamless process" | Describe the process honestly, including where friction typically occurs |
| "You should definitely..." | "The typical recommendation in this situation is..." |
| "Don't worry, it's easy" | Acknowledge the complexity, then show how we handle it |
| Vague risk language ("there can be complications") | Specific risk language ("if the title deed has an encumbrance, the transfer can be blocked for months") |
| Inspirational filler ("Cyprus awaits you!") | A specific, useful fact or next step |

## Quality Check

Before handing off any content, confirm:
- [ ] Voice is professional and human — not corporate, not casual
- [ ] Plain English used throughout; all technical terms explained on first use
- [ ] British English spelling throughout
- [ ] No expat clichés, lifestyle marketing, or sun/sea/sand framing
- [ ] Cause-and-effect structure used for process and risk content
- [ ] Reader addressed as "you" throughout
- [ ] Risks stated clearly but without scaremongering
- [ ] CTA is specific and low-friction — no "learn more" or "click here"
- [ ] One main message per piece, echoed throughout
- [ ] Humour, if used, is subtle and does not appear near legal/financial risk content
