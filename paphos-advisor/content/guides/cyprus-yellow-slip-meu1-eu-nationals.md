# Cyprus Yellow Slip (MEU1): Complete Guide for EU Nationals

*Last updated: April 2026*

If you are an EU, EEA, or Swiss national planning to live in Cyprus for more than three months, you are legally required to register with the Civil Registry and Migration Department and obtain a Registration Certificate — known informally as the Yellow Slip, or MEU1. This guide covers exactly what it is, who needs it, what documents you need to bring, and what to realistically expect at the office.

> **In this guide:**
> - What the Cyprus Yellow Slip is and who needs one
> - Which category you fall into (employed, self-sufficient, student)
> - The full document list, including the step many applicants miss
> - The real-world processing times by district in 2026
> - Common reasons applications are rejected and how to avoid them
> - What to do after you receive it

## What Is the Cyprus Yellow Slip?

The Cyprus Yellow Slip is the official registration certificate issued to EU, EEA, and Swiss nationals confirming their right to reside in Cyprus. Its formal name is the MEU1 Registration Certificate.

It is not a visa or a permit — EU citizens already have the legal right to live in Cyprus under EU freedom of movement rules. The Yellow Slip is proof that you have registered that right with the Cypriot authorities. Think of it as the formal record of your residency.

Once issued, the Yellow Slip does not expire and does not need to be renewed. You receive it once and keep it.

Why does it matter? Because the Yellow Slip is the foundational document that everything else in Cyprus builds on:

- **Banking:** Most banks require it to open a Cyprus resident account
- **Tax residency:** It is strong supporting evidence of physical presence for tax purposes
- **GESY (healthcare):** Required as part of the national healthcare system registration process
- **Vehicle re-registration:** You will need it to re-register a foreign vehicle in Cyprus
- **Company formation:** Required for EU nationals setting up a Cypriot company

If you are relocating to Cyprus, obtaining the Yellow Slip is the first process you should complete — not the last.

## Who This Applies To

The MEU1 applies to citizens of:

- All EU member states
- EEA countries: Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein
- Switzerland

You need a Yellow Slip if you are staying in Cyprus for more than three months. If you are a Cypriot citizen, you do not need one.

You will fall into one of four categories, which determines what proof you need to bring:

**Employed or self-employed in Cyprus**
You work for a Cyprus employer, or you are running a business registered in Cyprus. You need proof of your employment or business activity.

**Financially self-sufficient**
You are not working in Cyprus — you live off savings, rental income, a pension, dividends, or foreign employment income. You need to demonstrate you can support yourself without relying on the Cypriot social assistance system. You also need comprehensive health insurance valid in Cyprus.

**Student**
You are enrolled at a recognised educational institution in Cyprus. You need an enrolment letter plus evidence of sufficient financial resources and health insurance.

**Family member of an EU national already registered in Cyprus**
If your EU national spouse or family member is already registered, you can apply on that basis. Note: non-EU family members use a different form — MEU2. If you have a non-EU partner or children, they follow a separate process after your MEU1 is in place.

**A note on edge cases:**
- Dual nationals (EU + non-EU) should apply using their EU passport or national ID card
- Retirees with pension income typically fall under the self-sufficient category
- Remote workers employed abroad — common among digital nomads and EU nationals working for non-Cypriot employers — generally also fall under the self-sufficient category, but may need additional documentation to establish their financial basis clearly

## Documents Required

The document list varies slightly by category. The table below covers the most common cases — self-sufficient applicants and those employed in Cyprus.

| Document | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Valid EU passport or national ID card | Original + photocopy | Must be valid at time of application |
| Completed MEU1 application form | Original | Available at the CRMD office or online via the Cyprus government portal |
| 2 recent passport-sized photographs | Original | Standard biometric photo size |
| Proof of address in Cyprus | Original + photocopy | See note below — this is where most applications fail |
| Proof of financial resources or employment | Original + photocopy | Varies by category — see below |
| Comprehensive health insurance | Original + photocopy | Must cover Cyprus; see note on GESY below |
| €20 application fee | Cash | Paid at point of submission |

**Proof of address — the step most applicants miss:**
A rental agreement is the standard proof of address. Two requirements apply that are easy to get wrong:

1. The rental agreement must be for a minimum of 12 months. Short-term or holiday rental agreements are not accepted.
2. The rental agreement must be stamped at the Cyprus Tax Department (Tax Office). An unstamped rental agreement is one of the most common reasons applications are rejected at the desk. This is a separate step you must complete before attending the CRMD office — take the original rental agreement and a copy to your local Tax Office and have it stamped there first.

In some cases, a utility bill in the applicant's name has been accepted as an alternative to a rental agreement — but this is not guaranteed and varies by officer. If you are in this situation, confirm acceptability with your local CRMD office before attending.

**Proof of financial resources — by category:**

- *Employed in Cyprus:* Employment contract or a letter from your employer confirming your employment, plus recent payslips
- *Self-employed in Cyprus:* Certificate of registration with the Registrar of Companies or Tax Department, plus evidence of business activity
- *Self-sufficient:* Bank statements covering the last 6 months, showing income received and expenditure in Cyprus. There is no officially published minimum income threshold, but practitioners consistently report that officers expect to see net income of approximately €1,000–€1,500/month as a practical benchmark. This is not a legal minimum and may vary.
- *Student:* Enrolment letter from your institution plus evidence of financial support

**Health insurance:**
For applicants not yet registered with GESY (the Cypriot national healthcare system), private comprehensive health insurance valid in Cyprus is required. If you are employed in Cyprus and already contributing to GESY, some practitioners report that GESY proof is being accepted in place of private insurance — but this is not universal across all offices.

## Step-by-Step: How to Apply

### Step 1: Confirm your rental agreement is stamped

Before you do anything else, check your rental agreement is stamped at the Tax Department. If it is not stamped, go to your local Tax Office with the original and a photocopy and have it stamped first. This visit is separate from the CRMD and takes 15–30 minutes in most cases.

### Step 2: Download and complete the MEU1 form

The application form is available at the CRMD office or via the official Cyprus government portal at [gov.cy/moi/en/residence-cards/](https://www.gov.cy/moi/en/residence-cards/). Complete it in full before attending — do not leave fields blank.

### Step 3: Prepare your full document pack

Gather all documents per your category (see Documents Required above). Make photocopies of all originals. Bring more documentation than you think you need — having extra bank statements or supplementary letters costs nothing and avoids a second trip.

### Step 4: Check whether your district office requires an appointment

Some CRMD district offices operate on a walk-in basis; others require appointments. Paphos and Limassol are the busiest offices. Check the CRMD website or call ahead to confirm the process for your district.

### Step 5: Attend the CRMD district office in person

You must attend in person — a representative cannot submit the initial MEU1 application on your behalf. Bring originals and photocopies of all documents, the completed form, 2 passport photos, and €20 cash.

**Where to apply — by district:**

| District | Office |
|---|---|
| Paphos | CRMD Paphos District Office |
| Limassol | CRMD Limassol District Office |
| Nicosia | CRMD Nicosia District Office |
| Larnaca | CRMD Larnaca District Office |
| Famagusta | CRMD Famagusta District Office |

Submit your application to the relevant office for the district where you live.

### Step 6: Submit and pay

The officer will review your documents. If everything is in order, your application will be accepted and you will pay the €20 fee at the desk. If documents are missing, you will be sent away and need to return — which is why preparation matters.

### Step 7: Receive your Yellow Slip

In the best case, you receive the certificate on the same day or within a few days. In practice — particularly in Paphos and Limassol — you are likely to be given a receipt and told to return or wait for notification. Keep your receipt. See processing times below.

## Common Issues and How to Avoid Them

**Rental agreement not stamped at the Tax Office**
This is the most common reason for same-day rejection at the CRMD desk. The requirement is not prominently stated in all official guidance, but it is consistently enforced. Complete this step before attending the CRMD, not after.

**Rental agreement is for less than 12 months**
Short-term rental agreements — including holiday lets — are not accepted as proof of address. If your rental contract is for less than 12 months, you will need to renegotiate it before applying.

**Bank statements considered insufficient**
Ensure your statements cover the full 6 months prior to the application date, show both income and expenditure, and are in English (or accompanied by a certified Greek translation). Some officers request a formal bank letter in addition to statements. Include one if you have it.

**Applicant sent away without a clear explanation**
This happens. Officers vary in how clearly they communicate what is missing. Bring a comprehensive document checklist, bring slightly more than you think you need, and confirm the full requirements with a local lawyer or the CRMD office before attending.

**Certificate not issued on the day**
Common in Paphos and Limassol. This is not a rejection — you have been given a receipt and your application is being processed. Keep the receipt, note the expected timeframe given by the officer, and follow up if no confirmation is received after that period.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a Yellow Slip if I am only staying a few months?
No. The legal requirement applies if you intend to stay for more than three months. If you are staying for three months or less and are an EU national, you are exercising your right of free movement and no registration is required.

### Does the Yellow Slip expire?
No. The MEU1 Registration Certificate does not expire and does not need to be renewed. You receive it once and it remains valid indefinitely. Keep the original in a safe place — replacements require a new application.

### Can I start a bank account before I have my Yellow Slip?
Some banks will open an account for EU nationals with a valid passport before the Yellow Slip is issued, but many require it. If you are opening a Cypriot resident bank account, expect the Yellow Slip to be requested. Plan to apply for it early in your relocation timeline.

### Can I use a lawyer or agent to submit my application?
For the initial MEU1 application, you must attend the CRMD office in person. A representative cannot submit on your behalf. However, a lawyer or government liaison can help you prepare your documents correctly and follow up on progress after submission, which is often worth the cost in busy districts.

### I work remotely for a foreign company. Which category applies to me?
You most likely fall under the self-sufficient category, since your employer and income are not based in Cyprus. You will need to demonstrate sufficient financial resources via bank statements. Be prepared for the officer to ask for more documentation than the standard employed route — some remote workers bring an employment letter alongside their bank statements to show the source of their income clearly.

### What happens if my rental agreement is stamped but for less than 12 months?
You will need a new rental agreement for a minimum of 12 months, then have that stamped at the Tax Office before applying. There is no way around this requirement.
