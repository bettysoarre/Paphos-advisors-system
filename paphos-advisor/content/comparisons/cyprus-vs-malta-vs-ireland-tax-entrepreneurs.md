# Cyprus vs Malta vs Ireland — Tax Comparison for Entrepreneurs

*Last updated: April 2026*

If you are a business owner or entrepreneur considering where to base yourself and your company in Europe, Cyprus, Malta, and Ireland come up repeatedly as the shortlist. All three offer low corporate tax rates, EU membership, English as a working language, and relatively accessible routes to residency. But they are very different in how they work in practice — for you personally, for your company, and for your dividend income.

This comparison covers corporate tax, personal tax on dividends, IP regimes, residency requirements, and practical setup realities. It is written for entrepreneurs, founders, and investors — not accountants. If you are considering this decision seriously, you should take professional advice in each jurisdiction before acting.

> **In this comparison:**
> - Corporate tax rates and how they actually work in each country
> - What you pay on dividends as a shareholder-director
> - IP regimes and holding company treatment
> - What residency in each country actually requires
> - Which jurisdiction wins for which type of entrepreneur

## Cyprus

### How corporate tax works

Cyprus has a flat 12.5% corporate tax rate on net profits — one of the lowest in the EU. This applies to trading income. There is no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, which makes Cyprus a clean holding company jurisdiction.

### What you pay on your dividends

This is where Cyprus becomes compelling for business owners. If you are a Cyprus tax resident and qualify for Non-Dom status, you pay **0% Special Defence Contribution (SDC)** on dividends from your Cyprus company — for up to 17 years.

The only passive income levy that applies is the General Healthcare System (GHS) contribution at 2.65%, capped on income up to €180,000 per year. The maximum GHS on dividend income is therefore €4,770/year regardless of how much you take. Above €180,000 in dividends, the effective rate on the excess is 0%.

Combined with 12.5% corporate tax, the total effective tax on profits extracted as dividends is approximately **15.2% for the first €180,000**, and roughly **12.5%** beyond that. For business owners with significant dividend income, this is among the lowest in the EU.

### Tax residency requirements

Cyprus does not require you to be a full-time resident. Under the 60-day rule (updated January 2026), you qualify as a Cyprus tax resident if you:

- Spend at least 60 days in Cyprus in the calendar year
- Do not spend more than 183 days in any single other country
- Maintain a permanent home in Cyprus (owned or rented)
- Hold a Cyprus employment, directorship, or business activity through 31 December

As of 2026, Cyprus also removed the requirement that you must not be tax resident anywhere else. You can now hold dual tax residency and still qualify for the 60-day route — a significant expansion.

### IP and holding company

The Cyprus IP Box allows qualifying IP income to be taxed at an effective rate of **2.5%**. The holding company regime offers 0% withholding on outbound dividends and access to the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and an extensive double tax treaty network.

### Pros
- Very low effective rate on dividends under non-dom
- Minimal time requirement (60 days)
- Simple tax filing process
- Low cost of living — Paphos specifically offers a high quality of life at low cost
- 0% capital gains on securities disposal

### Cons
- Non-dom expires after 17 years of Cyprus tax residency
- Banking can be slow — opening a business bank account takes time
- Small economy — less talent pool and infrastructure than Dublin or Malta
- Proposed €5,000–€10,000 annual flat fee for new non-dom applicants (not yet enacted as of April 2026 — monitor)
- GHS contributions (2.65%) still apply even under non-dom

## Malta

### How corporate tax works

Malta's headline corporate tax rate is 35%, which looks alarming. The structure is designed differently: Malta operates a **full imputation system** where shareholders can claim a refund of most of the tax paid at company level. For a typical trading company, shareholders receive a 6/7 refund, reducing the effective corporate tax rate to approximately **5%**.

The catch: this requires going through a dividend refund process which takes time and requires proper Maltese accounting and administration. The headline rate is real — the refund comes back to shareholders, not to the company.

### What you pay on your dividends

Malta's non-dom regime is based on **remittance** — foreign-source income brought into Malta is taxed; income kept offshore is not. For entrepreneurs whose company income is structured correctly, this can result in a low personal tax rate on dividends. However, the structuring is more complex than Cyprus and requires specialist Maltese tax advice.

### Tax residency requirements

Malta requires 183 days of physical presence to establish tax residency, significantly more than Cyprus. Malta also has a Global Residence Programme and other structured schemes with minimum tax contributions (€15,000–€25,000/year) that provide a faster route for high earners, but these add to cost.

### IP and holding company

Malta has an 80% income deduction on qualifying IP income, resulting in an effective tax rate of approximately 6.25% — competitive but higher than Cyprus's 2.5%. Malta is also a well-established holding company jurisdiction with a strong treaty network.

### Pros
- Effective corporate rate can be very low with refund mechanism
- Well-established financial services sector
- Good English-language legal and accounting infrastructure
- EU jurisdiction with strong regulatory standing

### Cons
- The refund mechanism adds administrative complexity and cash flow timing issues
- 183-day presence requirement
- Minimum tax contributions under residence programmes add cost
- Higher cost of living than Cyprus
- Less suitable for simple founder structures — the refund system is designed for sophisticated setups

## Ireland

### How corporate tax works

Ireland has a 12.5% corporate tax rate on **trading income** — matching Cyprus. However, Ireland applies 25% on passive and non-trading income. From 2024, Ireland also applies the OECD Pillar Two minimum effective rate (15%) to large groups with revenue above €750m — this does not affect most entrepreneurs but signals the direction of travel.

Ireland's corporate tax regime is primarily designed for companies with genuine Irish substance — staff, office, operations. The OECD BEPS rules and increasing scrutiny from the EU make pure holding structures in Ireland more difficult to justify than in Cyprus.

### What you pay on your dividends

Ireland has no non-dom dividend exemption comparable to Cyprus. Dividends paid to Irish-resident shareholder-directors are subject to personal income tax at up to 40%, Universal Social Charge (USC) at up to 8%, and PRSI at 4% — a combined top rate approaching **52%**. [NEEDS SOURCE: confirm current Irish personal tax rates with an Irish tax specialist]

Ireland has a non-dom regime, but it operates on a remittance basis for foreign-source income — it does not provide a blanket exemption on dividends from Irish companies.

### Tax residency requirements

Ireland requires 183 days of physical presence (or 280 days over two consecutive years) to be considered tax resident. Dublin has a high cost of living — significantly higher than Cyprus or Malta.

### IP and holding company

Ireland's Knowledge Development Box (KDB) provides a 6.25% effective rate on qualifying IP income — the same as Malta. The holding company regime allows participation exemption on foreign dividends received. Ireland has an extensive treaty network and is widely used for holding structures, particularly by US-headquartered businesses.

### Pros
- 12.5% corporate tax on genuine trading activity
- Strong legal and financial services infrastructure
- Excellent talent pool — large English-speaking workforce
- Preferred by US multinationals — good for fundraising and exits
- Strong EU credibility

### Cons
- Very high personal tax rates on dividends — no comparable non-dom dividend exemption
- 183-day residency requirement
- High cost of living (Dublin among Europe's most expensive cities)
- Substance requirements increasingly enforced
- Less suitable for founders primarily motivated by personal tax efficiency

## Decision Guide

**Choose Cyprus if:**
- You want the lowest effective tax on dividends — the non-dom + 60-day combination is genuinely hard to beat
- You cannot or will not spend 183 days in one country — Cyprus's 60-day rule is unique in the EU
- You are a founder or investor primarily motivated by personal tax efficiency on dividend income
- You want simplicity — Cyprus has a lighter compliance burden than Malta or Ireland
- You value lifestyle — Paphos and Limassol offer a high quality of life at a fraction of Dublin or Malta's cost
- Your company does not need a large local talent base or sophisticated financial infrastructure

**Choose Malta if:**
- You run a financial services, iGaming, or crypto business — Malta has built specific regulatory frameworks for these
- You need an established holding company jurisdiction with a long track record
- Your tax adviser can manage the refund mechanism and you have the cash flow to handle timing
- You have complex offshore income structures where the remittance basis is advantageous

**Choose Ireland if:**
- You are raising institutional VC or PE funding — Irish structures are preferred by US investors
- You need a large English-speaking workforce and office infrastructure
- You are building a company that plans to exit via acquisition or IPO — Ireland is the established route for this
- Corporate tax efficiency matters more than personal dividend tax — the 12.5% rate on genuine trading is solid, but you will pay significant personal tax on what you take out
