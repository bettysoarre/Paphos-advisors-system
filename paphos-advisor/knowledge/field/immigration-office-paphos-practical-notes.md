# Paphos Immigration Office — Practical Notes

## What This Covers

Practical field intelligence for clients visiting the Paphos Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD) — the office that handles EU national registration documents including the Yellow Slip (MEU1) and Registration Certificate (MEU2). These notes cover how to prepare for a visit, what to expect on arrival, and how to avoid common pitfalls.

Does not cover the substantive documentation requirements for each process — those are in the relevant process docs (`PROC-IMM-001`). These notes cover the practical, operational reality of attending the office.

## How to Get an Appointment

Appointments for the Yellow Slip (MEU1) and Registration Certificate (MEU2) are required and must be booked through the official online booking system at [ariadni.moi.gov.cy](https://ariadni.moi.gov.cy).

Typical lead time from booking to appointment is 2–4 weeks. The booking system releases new slots on an ongoing basis — if no slots are available when you first check, try again in a few days or check early in the morning when new slots tend to appear.

Book as early as possible in the process — the appointment date is often the binding constraint, not document preparation.

---

## What to Expect on Arrival

Arrive 10–15 minutes before your appointment time. Bring the printed appointment confirmation or the reference number on your phone.

On arrival, go directly to the reception desk and give your name and appointment reference. You will be issued a ticket number and directed to a waiting area. Officers call names (not numbers) when they are ready.

Wait times from check-in to being seen are typically 15–45 minutes even with an appointment. Bring everything you need — there is no guarantee of access to a printer or photocopier on site.

## Practical Tips

- **Bring 2 copies of every document.** Officers sometimes retain the original and require a copy, or vice versa. Do not assume the office can photocopy for you.
- **Have your passport open at the photo page** and any entry stamps visible from the moment you approach the desk.
- **Do not separate documents into different bags or folders.** Officers work quickly and will ask for items in rapid succession — have everything in a single ordered stack.
- **Dress appropriately.** This is a formal government office. Some clients report being treated with more patience when dressed professionally.
- **Do not bring children unless unavoidable.** The waiting area has limited space and waits can be long.
- **Confirm all documents are originals unless copies are specified.** Officers will check. Missing an original is grounds for rejection on the day.
- **The officer at the desk makes the initial assessment but does not always have final sign-off.** If told a document is missing, ask specifically whether the application can still be accepted pending that document — sometimes it can.

## Known Issues and Workarounds

**Issue:** Appointment slots disappear quickly and can be hard to secure.
**Workaround:** Check the booking system at different times of day — new slots appear throughout the week. Early morning (08:00–09:00) is anecdotally when new availability is added.

**Issue:** Officers occasionally ask for a document not listed in the official requirements.
**Workaround:** Do not argue at the desk. Note what was requested, leave, obtain the document, and rebook. Escalating at the counter rarely helps and can make the interaction worse. If the request seems unreasonable or inconsistent with official guidance, seek advice before the next visit.

**Issue:** Clients arrive with the wrong type of photograph (wrong size, printed at home, printed on glossy paper).
**Workaround:** Use a professional photo shop for all passport-style photographs. Biometric photo standards apply — the office will reject non-compliant photos. Advise clients to get photos done the day before, not the same morning.

**Issue:** The booking system confirms an appointment but the office has no record of it on the day.
**Workaround:** Always bring a printed copy of the booking confirmation with the reference number. If there is no record, the printed confirmation is the evidence. In practice this is rare but has occurred.
