# Property FAQs

Covers renting and buying property in Cyprus — deposits, rental agreements, title deeds, off-plan purchases, and legal risks.

---

## Q: Can non-EU nationals rent property in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** Yes. There are no nationality restrictions on renting property in Cyprus. EU nationals, UK nationals, and non-EU nationals can all rent freely.

There are no nationality restrictions on renting residential property in Cyprus. EU nationals, UK nationals, and non-EU nationals (third-country nationals) can all rent freely without needing special permission or a specific visa status. The rental agreement process is the same for all nationalities. Note that while renting is unrestricted, the onward uses of the rental agreement vary by nationality: EU nationals use it for the MEU1 (Yellow Slip), while UK nationals and non-EU nationals use it as proof of address for their relevant residency permit applications.

## Q: How much deposit can a landlord charge in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** There is no legally set maximum. One to two months' rent is standard practice in Cyprus.

Cyprus law does not prescribe a maximum deposit amount for residential rental agreements. In practice, deposits of one to two months' rent are standard. Some landlords request two months for unfurnished properties or higher-value rentals. The deposit is held by the landlord — there is no formal deposit protection scheme in Cyprus. To protect yourself, document the condition of the property thoroughly when you move in (photographs, written inventory) and keep copies of all correspondence with the landlord. If a deposit is unjustifiably withheld, routes include direct negotiation, small claims court (amounts under €5,000), or district court for larger amounts.

## Q: Is stamp duty still payable on rental agreements in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** No. Stamp duty on rental agreements was abolished as of 1 January 2026. However, Tax Office registration may still be required for the Yellow Slip — confirm with your local CRMD office.

Stamp duty on rental agreements in Cyprus was abolished as of 1 January 2026. You no longer pay a fee when registering your rental agreement. However, the abolition created a point of confusion: the previous stamped rental agreement was both proof of stamp duty payment and a registration confirmation. With stamp duty gone, some CRMD offices have continued to request Tax Department registration as evidence that the contract is legitimate. As of April 2026, requirements vary by district. The safe approach is to visit your local Tax Office with the rental agreement and request a registration confirmation regardless — the process is quick (15–30 minutes) and now costs nothing.

## Q: What is the minimum rental term for a Yellow Slip application in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** 12 months. The CRMD requires a rental agreement for a minimum of 12 months as proof of permanent address for the Yellow Slip (MEU1) application.

To use a rental agreement as proof of permanent address for a Yellow Slip (MEU1) application, the agreement must be for a minimum of 12 months. Short-term lets, holiday rentals, and month-to-month agreements are not accepted by the CRMD as valid proof of permanent address. If your initial rental agreement is for less than 12 months, you will need to renegotiate or renew before submitting your Yellow Slip application. When negotiating a rental agreement, always secure a minimum 12-month initial term.

## Q: What is the riskiest stage of buying property in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** The reservation agreement. Signing it commits you to a purchase before full due diligence is complete. Never sign without a lawyer reviewing it first.

The reservation agreement is the highest-risk stage of a Cyprus property purchase. It is typically signed early in the process — often before a full title search, planning check, or mortgage encumbrance search has been completed — and it commits you to the purchase, usually requiring a non-refundable deposit of €1,000–€5,000 or more. Buyers who sign a reservation agreement without legal advice have very limited recourse if problems emerge during due diligence. Always instruct a Cyprus property lawyer before signing any reservation agreement, not after. Your lawyer should review the title deed status, check for planning violations, search for any mortgages or charges against the property, and confirm there are no outstanding property taxes or utility arrears.

## Q: Does a Cyprus title deed mean the property has no legal problems?

**Short answer:** No. A title deed confirms ownership but does not confirm the property is free of mortgages, building violations, or planning irregularities. A separate legal search is essential before any purchase.

A Cyprus title deed (certificate of registration) confirms that the property is registered in the seller's name at the Department of Lands and Surveys. It does not confirm: that there are no mortgages or charges against the property; that the property was built in compliance with its planning permit; that there are no illegal additions or violations; or that the property boundaries match what is on the ground. All of these require separate searches. Before completing a purchase, your Cyprus property lawyer should obtain a full title search to check for encumbrances, request a planning and building permit compliance check, verify there are no outstanding property tax arrears, and inspect the site against the registered plan. Many properties in Cyprus — particularly older ones built during the rapid development period of the 1980s to 2000s — have some form of planning irregularity. This does not necessarily prevent purchase, but must be known in advance.

## Q: What happens if my off-plan developer fails before completing my property in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** Off-plan buyers in Cyprus have specific performance rights — you can legally compel completion of the contract. Registering the contract with the Department of Lands and Surveys from the outset is essential to protect this position.

Cyprus law provides specific performance rights for buyers who have signed a purchase contract with a developer. If the developer becomes insolvent or fails to complete, your solicitor can apply to the court to enforce completion of the contract. These rights are strongest when the contract has been deposited with (stamped and registered at) the Department of Lands and Surveys — a critical step that some buyers skip, particularly on off-plan purchases where they delay registration. Ensure your lawyer deposits the contract immediately after signing. The practical process of enforcing specific performance against an insolvent developer can be lengthy and complex, but the legal right exists and has been successfully exercised by buyers in Cyprus. A good property lawyer will also advise on whether the developer carries adequate completion insurance or uses escrow arrangements.
