# Immigration FAQs

Covers Yellow Slip (MEU1), CRMD office practice, residency registration, and practical tips for dealing with Cyprus government offices.

---

## Q: Do I need a Yellow Slip if I am only staying in Cyprus for a few months?

**Short answer:** No. EU nationals staying for three months or less do not need to register. The Yellow Slip is required only for stays of more than three months.

EU, EEA, and Swiss nationals exercise their right of free movement for stays up to three months without needing to register. The Yellow Slip (MEU1) becomes legally required once you intend to stay for more than three months. If you are planning to establish Cyprus residency, tax residency, or open a bank account, you will need the Yellow Slip — apply early rather than waiting until the three-month threshold is reached.

---

## Q: Does the Cyprus Yellow Slip expire?

**Short answer:** No. The Yellow Slip (MEU1) does not expire and does not need to be renewed. You receive it once and it remains valid indefinitely.

The Cyprus Yellow Slip (MEU1 Registration Certificate) does not expire and never needs to be renewed. Once issued by the Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD), it remains valid for the duration of your residency in Cyprus. Keep the original in a safe place — if lost or damaged, you will need to apply for a replacement at the CRMD.

## Q: Does my rental agreement need to be stamped for the Cyprus Yellow Slip?

**Short answer:** Most CRMD offices still require Tax Department registration even after the 2026 stamp duty abolition. Visit the Tax Office before attending the CRMD.

Stamp duty on rental agreements was abolished in January 2026, but most CRMD district offices still request evidence of Tax Department registration as part of the Yellow Slip application. The safest approach is to take your rental agreement to your local Tax Office before attending the CRMD and obtain a registration confirmation — it costs nothing and takes 15–30 minutes. Practice varies by district, so confirm the current requirement with your local CRMD office before attending.

## Q: Can I use a hotel or serviced apartment as my address for a Yellow Slip application?

**Short answer:** No. The Yellow Slip requires a permanent address — a rental agreement or property ownership. A hotel or serviced apartment does not qualify.

A hotel, serviced apartment, or short-term rental does not qualify as a permanent address for Yellow Slip (MEU1) purposes. The CRMD requires a rental agreement for a residential property (minimum 12 months) or proof of property ownership. If you arrive in Cyprus and stay in temporary accommodation while searching for a property, you cannot submit your Yellow Slip application until you have a qualifying rental agreement in place. Most people allow 2–4 weeks to find and sign a property before beginning the application.

## Q: Can I open a Cyprus bank account before I have my Yellow Slip?

**Short answer:** Some banks will open accounts before the Yellow Slip is issued, but most require it for resident accounts. Plan your banking timeline around the Yellow Slip processing time.

Banking practice varies. Some banks will open accounts with a valid passport before the Yellow Slip is issued — particularly for non-resident accounts. For resident accounts, most banks require the Yellow Slip. Given that Paphos CRMD currently takes 1–3 months, plan your banking timeline accordingly. If urgent, ask your bank whether they can begin the process while the Yellow Slip application is pending.

## Q: I work remotely for a foreign company — which Yellow Slip category applies to me?

**Short answer:** You fall under the self-sufficient category. You will need 6 months of bank statements showing sufficient income — approximately €1,000–€1,500 per month is the informal threshold used by officers.

If your employer is based outside Cyprus, you are not employed in Cyprus and do not qualify under the employed category. You fall under the self-sufficient category, requiring 6 months of bank statements showing income and expenditure. There is no officially published minimum income threshold, but practitioners consistently report that officers expect approximately €1,000–€1,500 per month. Bring an employment letter alongside bank statements to show the source of your income clearly.

## Q: Will I need more documents than on the official checklist for my Cyprus immigration application?

**Short answer:** Possibly. Paphos CRMD officers sometimes request additional documents not on the official list. Bring common extras — extra bank statements, certified translations, and duplicate copies — as a precaution.

Immigration officers at the CRMD have some discretion over which documents they request at the counter. In practice, applicants sometimes encounter requests for documents not listed on the official guidance — additional bank statements, certified translations, or proof of employment beyond the minimum. This is more likely with borderline applications or when an officer wants extra assurance. To reduce the risk of being turned away: bring 6 months of bank statements even if 3 are specified; bring certified translations of all non-Greek and non-English documents; bring an extra copy of everything; and if possible, have a government liaison accompany you who knows current officer expectations at your specific CRMD office.

## Q: Is it normal and above board to use a government liaison in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** Yes. Using a professional with established government relationships is completely legal and is standard practice in Cyprus. Most businesses and relocating individuals use one.

In Cyprus, professional intermediaries — variously called government liaisons, fixers, or expeditors — work within the formal government process to facilitate applications on behalf of clients. This is legal, transparent, and widely used by individuals and businesses throughout Cyprus. A liaison attends the CRMD, Tax Department, Property Registry, or other offices on your behalf, uses established working relationships with officers to ensure paperwork is received correctly, and follows up through known channels. This is materially different from bribery or corruption — no payments are made to officers, and no rules are bypassed. UK clients in particular sometimes arrive with concerns about whether using a liaison is above board. It is, and it is the recommended approach for complex or time-sensitive applications in Cyprus.

## Q: Is there a better time of day to visit a Cyprus government office?

**Short answer:** Yes — morning. Government offices in Cyprus are generally more responsive before noon. Avoid afternoons and Fridays where possible.

Visiting government offices in Cyprus — including the CRMD, Tax Department, and Property Registry — before noon typically results in faster service. Offices are better staffed in the morning, queues are shorter, and officers are generally more receptive. Afternoons can see reduced staffing and longer waits, particularly in summer. If you have a choice of appointment time, book the earliest available slot. Avoid Fridays and the period immediately before public holidays, when offices are often understaffed or close early.

## Q: How soon can I follow up after submitting documents to a Cyprus government office?

**Short answer:** Wait at least 7 days. Contacting an officer sooner signals impatience and rarely produces useful information.

After submitting documents to a Cyprus government office, allow at least 7 calendar days before following up. Officers process applications in batches and handle multiple cases simultaneously. Contacting them before a week has passed is unlikely to produce any useful update and can create a negative impression. After 7 days, a brief and polite enquiry by phone or email is appropriate. If you are using a government liaison, they will typically manage the timing of follow-up contacts on your behalf based on their knowledge of that specific office's current workload.

## Q: How long should I wait before chasing a stalled Cyprus immigration application?

**Short answer:** Allow 15 working days (3 calendar weeks) before following up. Escalating earlier rarely helps and can create friction with the officer handling your case.

Once you have submitted an immigration application at the CRMD, allow at least 15 working days before following up on its status. Following up before this point is unlikely to produce useful information and can create friction with the officer handling your case — immigration offices operate on their own internal timeline and early chasing rarely accelerates processing. After 15 working days, you can make polite contact by phone or email, or ask a government liaison to enquire on your behalf. If there is still no movement after a further two weeks, a more direct follow-up is warranted.
