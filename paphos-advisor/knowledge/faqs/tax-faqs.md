# Tax FAQs

Covers the Cyprus 60-day tax residency rule, non-dom status, company tax structure, social insurance, and payment deadlines.

---

## Q: Can a digital nomad qualify under the Cyprus 60-day tax residency rule?

**Short answer:** Not without a Cyprus economic activity. Working remotely for a foreign employer does not satisfy the business activity condition — you need a Cyprus company directorship or Cyprus employment.

The 60-day rule requires economic activity in Cyprus — employment with a Cyprus employer, a directorship of a Cyprus company, or registered self-employment in Cyprus. Working remotely for a foreign company from Cyprus does not satisfy this condition. The most practical solution is to form a Cyprus company and take a directorship. This requires legal and tax advice to structure correctly, including ensuring the company has genuine substance.

---

## Q: Can I qualify for Cyprus tax residency if I am already tax resident in another country?

**Short answer:** Yes, as of 1 January 2026. Cyprus removed the condition requiring you to not be tax resident elsewhere, making the 60-day route available to those with dual tax residency.

As of 1 January 2026, Cyprus removed the condition that applicants under the 60-day rule must not be tax resident in any other country. You can now hold Cyprus tax residency while also being tax resident elsewhere. However, holding dual tax residency has implications — double tax treaties between Cyprus and the other country determine how your income is taxed, and tie-breaker rules may apply. Specialist cross-border tax advice is essential before acting.

## Q: Do both arrival and departure days count as days in Cyprus for the 60-day rule?

**Short answer:** Yes. Both the day you arrive in Cyprus and the day you depart count as full days in Cyprus, regardless of the time of travel.

Both the day of arrival and the day of departure are counted as days spent in Cyprus, regardless of time. A trip from Monday morning to Friday evening counts as five days. Keep accurate travel records throughout the year — boarding passes, hotel receipts, and a travel diary — as the Tax Department may request evidence if your day count is queried. Do not rely on bank transaction history alone.

## Q: Does my Cyprus company need to be actively trading to satisfy the 60-day rule business activity condition?

**Short answer:** Not legally. Holding a directorship is generally sufficient even if the company is not actively trading — but confirm the specific structure with a Cyprus tax specialist.

Multiple practitioner sources confirm that holding a directorship of a registered Cyprus company is generally sufficient to satisfy the economic activity condition, even if the company is not actively trading. The requirement is that the activity is maintained through 31 December of the tax year. However, the Tax Department may scrutinise arrangements that appear to exist solely to satisfy the residency test without genuine economic substance. Confirm with a Cyprus tax specialist.

## Q: Is Cyprus non-dom status automatic once I am a Cyprus tax resident?

**Short answer:** No. Non-dom status must be actively elected each year by filing form TD.38 with the Cyprus Tax Department alongside your annual tax return.

Non-dom status is not granted automatically when you become a Cyprus tax resident. You must actively elect it by filing form TD.38 (Declaration of Non-Domicile) with the Cyprus Tax Department, typically alongside your annual tax return. If you fail to file the TD.38 in a given year, SDC will apply to your dividend and interest income for that year. Ensure your accountant includes the TD.38 filing in your annual compliance process from day one.

## Q: Does Cyprus non-dom status exempt salary income from tax?

**Short answer:** No. Non-dom status exempts only dividend and passive interest income from SDC. Salary and active business income are still subject to Cyprus personal income tax at normal rates.

Non-dom status specifically exempts you from the Special Defence Contribution (SDC) on dividend income, passive interest income, and rental income from non-Cyprus property. It does not affect how salary or active business income is taxed. Salary in Cyprus is taxed under normal personal income tax rates (0% up to €19,500, progressive up to 35%). Most business owners structure their income as dividends from their Cyprus company to benefit from the non-dom exemption — a deliberate planning decision requiring advice from a Cyprus tax specialist.

## Q: Does the 17-year non-dom clock reset if I leave Cyprus and come back?

**Short answer:** No. The 17-year clock runs from your first year of Cyprus tax residency and does not reset if you leave and return.

The 17-year eligibility period runs from the first calendar year in which you were Cyprus tax resident — not from when you first claimed non-dom, and not from when you returned after a break. If you were Cyprus tax resident in 2010, left in 2015, and returned in 2022, the clock still started in 2010 and 5 years have been used. Years of Cyprus tax residency without a non-dom election still count against the clock. Verify your position with a Cyprus tax adviser if you have previous Cyprus residency history.

## Q: What happens to my non-dom status after 17 years of Cyprus tax residency?

**Short answer:** Non-dom status expires after 17 years. You become a deemed domicile and SDC applies at 17% on dividends and 3% on interest income.

Non-dom status in Cyprus is available for up to 17 consecutive years of Cyprus tax residency. Once you have been a Cyprus tax resident for 17 years, you are no longer eligible for non-dom status and become a deemed domicile — treated as domiciled in Cyprus for SDC purposes. SDC then applies at 17% on dividend income and 3% on interest and rental income. This is a significant change and should be planned for well in advance. Options include restructuring dividend timing, relocating assets, or adjusting your tax residency before the 17-year mark. Take specialist tax advice at least 2–3 years before your deadline.

## Q: What is the GHS contribution and does it apply to non-doms on dividend income?

**Short answer:** Yes. The GHS contribution of 2.65% on dividend income applies to non-doms, capped at €180,000 per year — maximum €4,770 per year. Non-dom status does not exempt you from GHS.

Cyprus non-dom status exempts dividend income from Special Defence Contribution (SDC) — but it does not exempt you from the General Healthcare System (GHS) contribution. The GHS levy of 2.65% applies to dividend income received by Cyprus tax residents, including non-doms. This contribution is capped at €180,000 of income per year, making the maximum GHS liability €4,770 per year regardless of how much dividend income you receive. Combined with 12.5% corporate tax, the total effective rate on profits extracted as dividends is approximately 15.2% for the first €180,000 of dividends, and 12.5% beyond that.

## Q: What records do I need to prove my days in Cyprus to the Tax Department?

**Short answer:** Keep flight records, boarding passes, hotel or rental receipts, and a travel diary. The Tax Department may request evidence of physical presence if your day count is queried.

Maintain: flight records and boarding passes for all arrivals and departures; hotel, Airbnb, or rental receipts on specific Cyprus dates; bank transaction records showing Cyprus spending; and a dated travel diary or spreadsheet. The Tax Department can challenge your day count if records are poor. Start keeping records from the beginning of the tax year — do not wait until filing time. Bank records alone are not always accepted as sufficient evidence of physical presence.

## Q: When do I pay income tax in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** In two equal instalments: 31 July and 31 December of the tax year. Missing a deadline incurs interest on the outstanding amount.

Cyprus personal income tax is paid on a self-assessment basis in two instalments during the tax year: the first instalment is due on 31 July, and the second on 31 December. Each instalment is half of the prior year's provisional tax assessment, or your own self-assessment if your income has changed significantly. The final tax liability is settled when you file your annual return (typically by 31 July of the following year). If instalments are not paid by the due dates, the Tax Department charges interest on the outstanding amount at the current statutory rate. Ensure your accountant sets up reminders for both instalment dates — the July deadline in particular catches some new residents by surprise.

## Q: What are the penalties for late or unpaid social insurance contributions in Cyprus?

**Short answer:** 3% per month on unpaid contributions, up to a maximum of 24%. Cases involving unregistered employees are referred to court rather than treated as a civil penalty.

Social insurance contributions in Cyprus are payable monthly by employers for all registered employees. If contributions are not paid by the due date, the Social Insurance Services impose a late penalty of 3% per month on the outstanding amount, accumulating up to a maximum of 24% of the original contribution owed. If employees are found to be working without being registered for social insurance — even for a short period — the case is referred to the courts rather than handled administratively. This can result in criminal prosecution of the employing company or its directors. Employers must register new employees with the Social Insurance Services on or before their first day of work.

## Q: Should I take a salary or dividends from my Cyprus company?

**Short answer:** Most owner-directors take a minimal salary and extract the rest as dividends. Under non-dom status, dividends are exempt from income tax. This is standard and legal tax planning.

For owner-directors of Cyprus companies who hold non-dom status, the most tax-efficient extraction structure is typically a minimal director salary combined with dividend distributions for the majority of remuneration. Under non-dom status, dividends are exempt from the Special Defence Contribution (SDC) at 17%, though GHS contributions of 2.65% (capped at €4,770 per year) still apply. The combined effective rate on profits extracted as dividends is approximately 15.2% for the first €180,000 of dividends (12.5% corporate tax plus 2.65% GHS), versus income tax rates of up to 35% on salary. The minimal salary threshold must be agreed with your accountant — it should be commercially justifiable and reflect genuine management activity. This structure is widely used, well-established in Cyprus practice, and is not a grey area.
