## Overview

Renting a property is typically the first practical step for anyone relocating to Cyprus. A rental agreement provides proof of address — which is required for the Yellow Slip (MEU1), tax residency registration, bank account opening, vehicle registration, and GESY healthcare registration. The Cyprus rental market is relatively straightforward but has specific legal requirements that relocators commonly miss — particularly around contract stamping, deposit protections, and minimum rental terms. As of January 2026, stamp duty on rental agreements has been abolished, simplifying one part of the process.

---

## Eligibility

There are no nationality restrictions on renting property in Cyprus. EU nationals, UK nationals, and third-country nationals can all rent freely. The landlord sets their own qualifying criteria (income, references, etc.).

---

## Required Documents

Documents typically required by a landlord or agent when applying to rent:

| Document | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Valid passport or ID | For identity verification |
| Proof of income or financial means | Bank statements, employment contract, or pension statement — landlord's discretion |
| References | Previous landlord reference or employer reference — not always required |
| Deposit (1–2 months' rent) | Paid on signing; held against damages |
| First month's rent | Typically paid on signing |

**Documents the tenant should request from the landlord:**
- Proof of ownership (title deed or Land Registry extract) — confirm the landlord owns or is authorised to let the property
- Any existing mortgage details — if the property is mortgaged, confirm the mortgage does not prohibit letting

## Where to Apply

There is no government office involved in renting a property. The process is between tenant and landlord (or agent). For finding properties:

- Property portals: Bazaraki.com is the dominant classified listing site in Cyprus
- Local property agents — particularly useful in Paphos and Limassol where competition for good properties is high
- Facebook groups for expats in Cyprus — significant informal rental market, particularly for furnished short-term lets

---

## Process Steps

1. **Search for properties.** Use Bazaraki.com, a local property agent, or expat community groups. Define your requirements: district, budget, furnished/unfurnished, number of bedrooms, garden, pool, proximity to schools or offices.

2. **View properties in person.** Never pay any money before a physical viewing. Rental scams exist — listings on Bazaraki and Facebook may not represent real properties. Verify the property exists and is as described before proceeding.

3. **Verify the landlord's ownership.** Before signing, ask to see the title deed or a Land Registry extract confirming the landlord owns the property or is authorised to let it. A lawyer can run a title search for a small fee if needed.

4. **Negotiate terms.** Agree on: monthly rent, length of tenancy, deposit amount, what is included (furniture, white goods, utilities), and any rent review provisions.

5. **Review the rental agreement carefully.** Ensure the contract includes:
   - Names and details of both parties
   - Full property description and address
   - Rental amount and payment terms
   - Deposit amount and conditions for return
   - Tenancy duration and renewal terms
   - Termination notice periods (typically 1–3 months)
   - Maintenance responsibilities
   - Inventory of contents if furnished
   - Whether subletting is permitted (usually prohibited)

6. **Sign the rental agreement.** Both parties sign. You receive a copy. Keep the original safe — you will need it for MEU1 registration, bank accounts, and tax purposes.

7. **Pay deposit and first month's rent.** Pay by bank transfer where possible — this creates a paper trail. Get a receipt.

8. **Register utilities in your name.** Contact the Electricity Authority of Cyprus (EAC), the local water board, and if applicable, a gas supplier to transfer or register accounts in your name. See PROC-SET-001.

9. **Use the rental agreement for official registrations.** The signed rental agreement (minimum 12-month term) is required for Yellow Slip (MEU1) registration and for tax residency establishment. Ensure the contract is for at least 12 months before using it for these purposes.

---

## Processing Time

- Viewing to signing: typically 1–7 days in a normal market; faster in peak season if the property is in demand
- Utilities transfer: 1–5 working days per utility
- No government processing time — the rental agreement is a private contract

## Field Notes

> **These notes reflect field intelligence from practitioner sources and community reports. They are not official guidance.**

**FN-001 — Stamp duty abolished from 1 January 2026**
Rental agreements entered into from 1 January 2026 are no longer subject to stamp duty following the repeal of the Stamp Duty Law. This removes the previous requirement to have the contract stamped at the Tax Office. However — the MEU1 application (Yellow Slip) previously required a Tax Office-stamped rental agreement. Confirm with the local CRMD office whether a stamped contract is still required for MEU1 purposes following the abolition of stamp duty, or whether a standard signed contract is now sufficient.
*Source: Cyprus Mail, March 2026; ICLG Real Estate Report 2026.*

**FN-002 — Paphos rental market is very competitive for quality long-term lets**
In Paphos, well-priced, well-maintained 3-bedroom properties let quickly — often within days of listing. Good properties at fair prices are rarely available for more than a week. Relocators should be prepared to move fast once they find a suitable property, and should avoid the mistake of spending too long searching from abroad without a local agent.
*Source: Field intelligence, April 2026.*

**FN-003 — Minimum 12-month term is required for MEU1 purposes**
Even though shorter-term rentals are legally valid in Cyprus, the CRMD requires the rental agreement used for MEU1 registration to cover at least 12 months. If you are signing a shorter lease (e.g. 6 months) as a transitional arrangement, this cannot be used for Yellow Slip registration. Ensure your first formal contract is for at least 12 months.
*Source: Practitioner guidance, April 2026.*

**FN-004 — Eviction is extremely slow through the courts**
If a landlord-tenant dispute escalates to eviction proceedings, the process through the Cyprus courts can take up to 5 years. This is rarely relevant for well-screened tenants but means landlords are cautious and may apply stricter screening. Tenants should be aware that a landlord cannot simply evict them because they want to sell — the tenancy agreement must be honoured or terminated per its terms.
*Source: Cyprus Mail, AGP Law, 2026.*

**FN-005 — Furnished vs unfurnished pricing dynamics**
Furnished properties command a premium of 15–30% over unfurnished equivalents in Paphos and Limassol. For longer-term relocators (2+ years), unfurnished and buying furniture locally is often more economical. Short-term and transitional lets are almost exclusively furnished.
*Source: La Maison Estates guide, April 2026.*

## Common Issues

**Issue:** Landlord asks for a cash deposit with no receipt and no written agreement.
**Resolution:** Never pay cash without a receipt and a written contract. A verbal rental agreement has limited legal enforceability in Cyprus. Always insist on a written contract before paying anything.

**Issue:** Rental agreement is for less than 12 months — rejected for MEU1 registration.
**Resolution:** Negotiate a minimum 12-month contract from the start. If your landlord only offers 6 months, use that as a bridge while you find a longer-term property, but do not use it for official registrations.

**Issue:** Landlord refuses to return deposit at end of tenancy.
**Resolution:** Document the property's condition at move-in with photographs. Keep all payment receipts. If the landlord refuses to return the deposit without justification, you can pursue a claim through the Cyprus Rent Control Court or civil courts. For amounts under €5,000, the small claims process is available.

**Issue:** Property is listed by someone who does not own it (rental scam).
**Resolution:** Before paying any money, ask to see the title deed and verify the landlord's identity matches. A lawyer can conduct a Land Registry title search for a small fee. Never send money abroad or pay before a physical viewing.

**Issue:** Utilities are still in the previous tenant's name and the landlord asks you to sort it out.
**Resolution:** Contact EAC and the local water board directly to transfer the account. You will need your rental agreement, passport, and deposit. This is routine and can usually be done in person or online within a few days.

## Related Processes

- **PROC-IMM-001 — Yellow Slip Application (MEU1):** The rental agreement (minimum 12 months) is a required document for MEU1 registration. Renting is typically the first step before MEU1 can be completed.
- **PROC-TAX-001 — 60-Day Tax Residency Rule:** The rental agreement is evidence of a "permanent home available in Cyprus" — one of the qualifying conditions for tax residency under the 60-day rule.
- **PROC-SET-001 — Utilities Registration:** Registering utilities in your name follows immediately after signing the rental agreement.
