## Summary

All self-employed individuals in Cyprus — including sole traders, freelancers, and company directors who draw a salary — must register with the Social Insurance Services and make monthly contributions. Social insurance is separate from GeSY (though linked administratively for self-employed). Registration must happen **before commencing activity** — penalties for late registration include a 3% per month surcharge on unpaid contributions, up to a maximum of 24%, and unregistered employment cases are referred to the courts. Social insurance entitles contributors to sickness benefit, maternity pay, pension accrual, and unemployment benefit under certain conditions.

---

## Who This Applies To

- **Self-employed individuals** trading in Cyprus (sole traders, freelancers, consultants)
- **Company directors drawing a salary** from their Cyprus company — the company registers and pays contributions on the director's salary
- **Non-EU work permit holders** — employer handles registration; see PROC-IMM-009 for employer obligations

**Company directors extracting only dividends (no salary):** No social insurance registration is required for dividend-only extraction. Social insurance applies only to earned income (salary). Dividend-only extraction is common among non-dom directors — confirm structure with your accountant.

## The Process

1. **Obtain TIC first** (PROC-SET-004) — social insurance registration is linked to your TIC
2. **Register before commencing activity** — registration is required from the first day of self-employment
3. **Visit the Social Insurance Services district office** or register online via the Social Insurance portal [NEEDS VALIDATION — confirm if online registration is available]
4. **Complete and submit application** with supporting documents
5. **Receive registration confirmation** — keep this for your accountant's records
6. **Begin monthly contributions** — contributions are calculated quarterly or annually depending on income type and paid to the Social Insurance Services; your accountant will manage the schedule

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

| Breach | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Late contribution payment | 3% per month on outstanding amount, up to 24% maximum |
| Unregistered self-employment discovered | Referred to court (not administrative) — criminal prosecution possible |
| Employer failure to register an employee | Same penalties apply; employer is liable, not employee |

These penalties are material — always advise clients to register before their first day of business activity, not after.

---

## Social Insurance Entitlements

Once contributing, self-employed individuals accrue entitlements:

| Benefit | Condition |
|---|---|
| Sickness benefit | After minimum contribution weeks; partial income replacement during illness |
| Maternity benefit | After minimum contribution weeks; for self-employed mothers |
| Old age pension | Accrues with years of contribution; amount based on contribution history |
| Unemployment benefit | Generally not available to self-employed (applies to employees made redundant) |
| Industrial injuries benefit | Applicable in theory; rare for office-based self-employed |

## Notes for Agents

- The most common mistake is a client forming a Cyprus company and beginning to operate without registering for social insurance. The "I hadn't started billing yet" defence is not accepted — registration is required from the date of commencement of activity, not the date of first invoice.
- Directors who pay themselves a salary (even a minimal one) trigger both social insurance and ERGANI obligations. Dividend-only directors avoid this, but the structure must be set up correctly. This is an accountant conversation.
- Work permit holders: social insurance is the employer's responsibility, not the employee's. If an employer fails to register, the employee is exposed despite having done nothing wrong. Flag this risk to employer clients.
