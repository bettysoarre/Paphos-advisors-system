## Summary

Opening a Cyprus bank account is one of the first practical steps for any new resident or company. Personal accounts typically take 1–4 weeks; corporate accounts take 4–12 weeks due to thorough KYC and AML checks. The main banks are Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank, and Alpha Bank. All three require a residency document (Yellow Slip, Pink Slip, ARC card, or equivalent) for resident accounts. Until the residency document is issued, some banks allow a non-resident account to be opened on a valid passport — useful for bridging the gap. For Cyprus companies, the bank account is typically the longest part of the formation process and should be started immediately after incorporation.

---

## Who This Applies To

- **All new residents** needing a Cyprus personal bank account for day-to-day living, salary receipt, or tax compliance
- **Cyprus companies** (new incorporations) needing a corporate current account to operate
- **Non-resident individuals** with regular Cyprus financial activity (property purchase, company payments)

## What You Will Need

### Personal Account — Documents

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Valid passport | Original required |
| Residency document | Yellow Slip (MEU1), Pink Slip, ARC card, Digital Nomad card, or equivalent |
| Proof of Cyprus address | Utility bill or rental agreement — typically last 3 months |
| Source of funds declaration | Informal at most banks; some require supporting documents |
| Tax Identification Code (TIC) | Obtain from Cyprus Tax Department first (see PROC-SET-004) |
| Foreign bank statements | 3–6 months; required by some banks for higher-value accounts |

**Without residency document:** Some banks open non-resident accounts on passport + proof of address only. Confirm with the specific branch before visiting.

### Corporate Account — Documents

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Certificate of Incorporation | Issued by Cyprus Registrar of Companies |
| Memorandum and Articles of Association | Full document, certified |
| Certificate of Directors and Secretary | Current, recently dated |
| Certificate of Registered Address | Current |
| Certificate of Shareholders | Current — full ownership structure |
| Passports of all directors and beneficial owners | Certified copies |
| Proof of address for all directors and beneficial owners | Utility bills or bank statements, last 3 months |
| Business description | 1–2 pages covering business activity, clients, revenue model |
| Source of funds — company | Bank statements, invoices, or contracts showing where company income comes from |
| Personal bank statements for directors | 3–6 months — demonstrates personal source of funds |

**Lawyer introduction:** A Cyprus lawyer or accountant who already has a relationship with the bank can introduce the application, reducing processing time and the risk of a request for additional information.

## The Process

### Personal Account

1. **Obtain TIC first** (PROC-SET-004) — most banks require it before opening a resident account
2. **Choose a bank** — Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank, or Alpha Bank are the main options; each has different minimum balance requirements and fee structures
3. **Book an appointment** — most banks require an in-person visit; some branches in Paphos accept walk-ins
4. **Attend with full document pack** — bring originals and copies of all documents above
5. **Wait for KYC approval** — typically 1–2 weeks; the bank may call with follow-up questions
6. **Receive account details and card** — debit card typically arrives within 5–10 working days of approval

### Corporate Account

1. **Complete company incorporation** (PROC-BIZ-001) before approaching a bank
2. **Prepare a comprehensive KYC pack** — the fuller the initial submission, the fewer follow-up requests
3. **Use a lawyer or accountant introducer** — strongly recommended; reduces risk of rejection or extended delays
4. **Submit to 2–3 banks simultaneously** — if one rejects or takes too long, you have alternatives in progress
5. **Respond promptly to follow-up requests** — KYC queries that go unanswered for 2+ weeks reset the queue
6. **Timeline: budget 4–12 weeks** from submission to a working account

## Timeline

| Account Type | Typical Duration | Key Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|
| Personal (resident) | 1–3 weeks | Residency document availability |
| Personal (non-resident) | 1–2 weeks | Source of funds queries |
| Corporate | 4–12 weeks | KYC pack completeness; source of funds |
| Corporate (complex ownership) | Up to 6 months | Multi-jurisdiction beneficial ownership |

---

## Interim Solutions While Waiting

For companies waiting for a Cyprus bank account, Electronic Money Institution (EMI) accounts (Wise Business, Revolut Business) can receive payments and pay suppliers. They are not bank accounts and may not be accepted by all counterparties — confirm with your accountant before relying on an EMI account as your primary corporate account.

## Common Issues

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Application rejected — business type | Crypto companies will be rejected by all three major banks. See KA-FLD-013. Use EMI accounts. |
| Application delayed by source of funds query | Provide bank statements, contracts, or tax returns showing origin of funds — the more documentation the better |
| Non-resident account refused | Try a different bank or branch; policy varies |
| Corporate account: complex ownership structure | Engage a lawyer who knows which bank has the lowest barrier for your structure |

---

## Notes for Agents

- The bank account is consistently the longest element of company formation. Flag this to clients on day one — starting the banking process immediately after incorporation is standard practice.
- September and January are particularly slow at Paphos branches due to volume. Corporate account applications should allow a 3-month buffer from submission to first working day.
- Clients with prior banking in Cyprus (even closed accounts) often get through KYC faster if they can provide their old account details.
