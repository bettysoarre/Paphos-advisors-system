## Summary

The 60-Day Rule is an alternative route to becoming a Cyprus tax resident that requires spending a minimum of 60 days in Cyprus during a calendar year — significantly fewer than the standard 183-day threshold. It was introduced to attract internationally mobile individuals and business owners who cannot commit to spending the majority of the year in one country. As of January 2026, Cyprus removed the previous requirement that applicants must not be tax resident in any other country, making the 60-day route available to a much wider group. Establishing Cyprus tax residency is the prerequisite for accessing the non-dom regime and its associated tax exemptions.

---

## Who This Applies To

Any individual may qualify for Cyprus tax residency under the 60-day rule provided **all** of the following conditions are met:

- **Minimum 60 days in Cyprus** during the calendar year (does not need to be consecutive)
- **No more than 183 days in any single other country** during the same calendar year
- **Permanent home available in Cyprus** throughout the year — owned or rented; must be genuinely available, not just nominally held
- **Employment, directorship, or business activity in Cyprus** that is maintained through 31 December of the tax year — this can be a directorship of a Cyprus company, employment with a Cyprus-registered employer, or active business operations in Cyprus

**As of 1 January 2026:** The previous condition requiring the individual to NOT be tax resident in any other country has been removed. Individuals who are also tax resident elsewhere can now qualify under the 60-day rule in Cyprus.

**Note:** The standard 183-day rule also exists — anyone spending more than 183 days in Cyprus in a calendar year automatically becomes a Cyprus tax resident regardless of the above conditions.

**Edge cases:**
- Days of arrival and departure are both counted as days in Cyprus
- Brief exits (holidays, business trips) do not break the residency — the total days in Cyprus must reach 60
- Digital nomads and remote workers with no Cyprus business activity cannot qualify under the 60-day rule unless they establish a Cyprus company or take employment here

## What You Will Need

### Documents Required

There is no single application form for the 60-day rule — tax residency is established by registering with the Cyprus Tax Department and providing supporting evidence of the qualifying conditions.

| Document | Purpose | Notes |
|----------|---------|-------|
| Passport / ID with entry/exit stamps or travel records | Proof of days in Cyprus | Flight records, boarding passes, hotel receipts, or a formally kept travel log |
| Rental agreement or title deed | Proof of permanent home in Cyprus | Rental agreement must cover the full calendar year; must be stamped at Tax Office |
| Employment contract / directorship letter / company registration | Proof of Cyprus business/employment activity | Must show activity through 31 December of the tax year |
| Completed TD.1 form (Tax Return) | Annual tax filing | Required once tax residency is established |
| TIC (Tax Identification Code) application | Registration with Tax Department | Required before filing; applied for at the local Tax Department office |

### Where to Apply

Tax residency under the 60-day rule is established through the **Cyprus Tax Department**.

- **Online portal:** [https://www.taxisnet.gov.cy](https://www.taxisnet.gov.cy) — for TIC registration and annual tax return filing
- **Local Tax Department offices** — for in-person TIC registration and queries; one in each district
- An accountant or tax specialist can handle registration and filing on your behalf with a power of attorney

---

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Establish the qualifying conditions during the calendar year.** Ensure you spend at least 60 days in Cyprus, do not spend more than 183 days in any other single country, have a home available in Cyprus all year, and hold a Cyprus employment, directorship, or business activity through 31 December.

2. **Keep records of days in Cyprus throughout the year.** Retain flight records, boarding passes, hotel receipts, or a travel diary. The Tax Department may request evidence of physical presence.

3. **Register for a Tax Identification Code (TIC).** If you do not already have one, apply at the local Tax Department office or via the Taxisnet portal. Bring your passport and proof of Cyprus address. This can be done at any point during the year.

4. **Register on the Taxisnet portal.** Create an account on the Cyprus Tax Department's online portal at taxisnet.gov.cy. This is required for filing your annual tax return.

5. **File your annual tax return (TD.1).** Tax returns for a given year are filed the following year — the exact deadline varies (typically July–October). Your tax residency is formally established through the filing. A tax specialist or accountant should prepare the return.

6. **Request a Cyprus Tax Residency Certificate if required.** If you need to prove Cyprus tax residency to a foreign tax authority or bank, request a certificate from the Tax Department after filing your return.

---

### Timeline

- **TIC registration:** Same day or within a few days at the local office
- **Tax residency certificate:** Typically issued within 2–4 weeks of request, after the annual return has been filed
- **Annual tax return deadline:** Varies by year — typically July–October for the prior year. Confirm the current deadline with a tax specialist

## Field Notes

> **These notes reflect field intelligence from practitioner sources. They are not official guidance and should be verified with a qualified tax specialist.**

**FN-001 — 2026 rule change: no-other-tax-residency condition removed (April 2026)**
As of 1 January 2026, Cyprus dropped the requirement that applicants under the 60-day rule must not be tax resident in any other country. This was confirmed by multiple sources including VisaHQ (April 7, 2026) and practitioner guides. This is a significant expansion of who can use the 60-day route. Individuals who previously could not qualify because they remained tax resident in the UK, US, or elsewhere can now consider Cyprus tax residency under the 60-day rule.
*Source: VisaHQ news April 2026, GK Law Firm guide April 2026. Needs validation by a Cyprus tax specialist.*

**FN-002 — Proposed annual flat fee for new non-dom applicants (2026)**
There is a proposal under discussion to introduce an annual flat fee of €5,000–€10,000 for new non-dom applicants, potentially replacing aspects of the 60-day rule. This had not been formally enacted as of April 2026. Monitor for legislative updates.
*Source: Practitioner guides, April 2026. Status: proposed, not yet law.*

**FN-003 — Cyprus company directorship is the most common qualifying business activity**
The most commonly used method to satisfy the "Cyprus business activity" condition is holding a directorship of a Cyprus-registered company. This does not require the company to be actively trading in Cyprus — being a director is typically sufficient. A tax specialist should confirm this is structured correctly to withstand scrutiny.
*Source: Multiple practitioner guides, April 2026.*

**FN-004 — Travel records are critical and often underprepared**
The Tax Department can request evidence of physical presence in Cyprus. Many applicants do not keep formal records throughout the year and struggle to reconstruct their day count retrospectively. Keep a travel diary or spreadsheet updated throughout the year, supported by flight records and receipts.
*Source: Practitioner guidance, April 2026.*

## Common Issues and How to Handle Them

**Issue:** Applicant fails the 60-day count because they counted travel days incorrectly.
**Resolution:** Both the day of arrival and day of departure count as days in Cyprus. Count conservatively and keep records from the start of the year.

**Issue:** Applicant has a home in Cyprus but it is a holiday rental that is let out for parts of the year.
**Resolution:** The home must be genuinely available to the applicant throughout the year. A property that is rented to third parties during peak seasons may not qualify. Seek tax advice on how to structure this correctly.

**Issue:** Digital nomad working remotely with no Cyprus business activity cannot satisfy the employment/business condition.
**Resolution:** The most practical solution is to establish a Cyprus company and take a directorship. This requires legal and tax advice to structure correctly.

**Issue:** Foreign tax authority disputes Cyprus tax residency claim.
**Resolution:** Obtain a Cyprus Tax Residency Certificate from the Tax Department. Maintain comprehensive records of days in Cyprus and all qualifying conditions throughout the year.
