## Summary

Incorporating a Cyprus Private Limited Company (Ltd) is the standard route for entrepreneurs, founders, and investors establishing a business presence in Cyprus. The process is handled through the Cyprus Registrar of Companies and typically completed within 5–10 working days for standard registration, or 1–3 days under the expedited service. A Cyprus company is commonly used to satisfy the "business activity" condition of the 60-day tax residency rule, to hold IP under the 2.5% IP Box regime, and to structure dividend extraction at a combined effective rate of approximately 12.5–15.2% for non-dom residents. In practice, the most time-consuming step is opening a corporate bank account, which can take 4–12 weeks or more.

---

## Who This Applies To

Any individual or corporate entity may form a Cyprus Private Limited Company. There are no nationality restrictions on directors or shareholders — non-residents and non-EU nationals may form and own Cyprus companies.

**Key eligibility notes:**
- Minimum 1 director required (can be non-resident, any nationality)
- Minimum 1 shareholder required (can be a corporate entity)
- A company secretary must be appointed (must be Cyprus-resident or a Cyprus-licensed service provider)
- A registered office address in Cyprus is required
- No minimum share capital requirement for private limited companies [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm current minimum share capital rules with a Cyprus lawyer]

**This process covers:**
- First-time company formation by an individual entrepreneur or investor
- Formation by an existing foreign company establishing a Cyprus subsidiary or holding company

**Out of scope:**
- Public limited companies (PLCs)
- Branch offices of foreign companies
- Partnerships
- Conversion of existing entities

### Where to Apply

Registration is handled through the **Cyprus Registrar of Companies and Intellectual Property (DRCOR)**:

- **eFiling portal:** [https://efiling.drcor.mcit.gov.cy](https://efiling.drcor.mcit.gov.cy) — online submission for most filings
- **In-person:** DRCOR offices in Nicosia (head office), Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos, and Famagusta
- **Via a Cyprus lawyer:** The standard route — lawyers submit on behalf of clients and manage the entire process

In practice, virtually all company formations are handled by a Cyprus lawyer or licensed formation agent. The eFiling portal is used by the lawyer, not directly by the client.

| Name availability check | 1–3 working days |
| Standard company registration | 5–10 working days |
| Expedited company registration | 1–3 working days |
| TIC (tax identification) registration | 1–5 working days |
| VAT registration (if applicable) | 2–4 weeks |
| Corporate bank account opening | 4–12 weeks (often longer) |

**The bank account is the long pole.** The company is legally registered quickly, but operating it requires a bank account, and Cyprus banking KYC is thorough and slow. Budget 2–3 months from the start of the process to having a functioning bank account. Some clients use EMI (electronic money institution) accounts (e.g. Wise Business, Revolut Business) as a faster interim solution — confirm acceptability with your accountant.

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1: Choose and reserve your company name

Instruct your Cyprus lawyer to check name availability with the Registrar of Companies. Names must be unique and not misleading. Avoid names that imply government connection or regulated activity (e.g. "Bank", "Insurance") without appropriate licensing.

Name reservation confirms availability but is not formal registration — proceed immediately to the next step.

**Timeline:** 1–3 working days.

---

### Step 2: Define company structure

Before the M&AA is drafted, decide on:
- **Directors** — who will be appointed (you, a local nominee, or both)
- **Shareholders** — individual(s) or a holding company
- **Share capital** — amount and class of shares to be issued
- **Company objects** — the business activities the company will carry out (broadly drafted M&AAs are standard)

**Tax substance note:** For Cyprus to be recognised as the company's tax residence (rather than the residence of the controlling directors), it is advisable to have at least one Cyprus-resident director, or to ensure board decisions are made in Cyprus. Discuss this with your tax adviser before finalising the structure. This matters if you plan to use the 12.5% corporate rate and non-dom dividend benefits.

---

### Step 3: Instruct a Cyprus lawyer to prepare and file incorporation documents

The lawyer prepares:
- Memorandum and Articles of Association
- HE1, HE2, HE3 forms
- Consent and subscriber documents

All directors and shareholders must provide certified passport copies and proof of address. The lawyer submits the full package to the Registrar via the eFiling portal or in person.

**Timeline:** Document preparation 2–5 days; submission to Registrar immediately after.

---

### Step 4: Registrar processes the application

The Registrar reviews the submission. If everything is in order, the company is registered and a **Certificate of Incorporation** is issued. If there are queries or rejected documents, the lawyer handles responses.

Standard processing: 5–10 working days. Expedited: 1–3 working days (additional fee applies).

---

### Step 5: Receive incorporation documents

Once registered, you receive:
- **Certificate of Incorporation** — confirms the company is legally registered
- **Certificate of Registered Office** — confirms the Cyprus address
- **Certificate of Directors and Secretary** — lists appointed officers
- **Certificate of Shareholders** — lists the share structure
- **M&AA** — your company's constitutional document

Keep originals securely. Certified copies are required for banking and other processes.

---

### Step 6: Register the company with the Cyprus Tax Department

Register for a **Tax Identification Code (TIC)** with the Tax Department via the Taxisnet portal or at your local Tax Department office. This is required for filing corporate tax returns and is needed before the bank account application.

If your expected annual turnover exceeds €15,600, also register for **VAT** with the Tax Department. [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm current VAT threshold]

**Timeline:** TIC: 1–5 days. VAT registration: 2–4 weeks.

---

### Step 7: Open a corporate bank account

This is the most time-consuming step. Apply to a Cyprus bank (Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank, Alpha Bank) or an EMI. You will need:
- Certificate of Incorporation and all company certificates
- Certified M&AA
- Certified passport copies and proof of address for all directors and beneficial owners
- Business plan or description of intended business activity
- Expected transaction volume and source of funds
- Personal bank statements for directors/beneficial owners (typically 3–6 months)

The bank conducts full KYC and AML checks. Expect requests for additional documentation. Processing time: 4–12 weeks, sometimes longer for complex structures or non-EU shareholders.

**Interim option:** Some clients use Wise Business or Revolut Business as an EMI account while waiting for a full bank account. Confirm whether this is acceptable for your specific use case (e.g. invoicing, receiving dividends) with your accountant.

---

### Step 8: Register for social insurance (if employing staff)

If the company will employ staff (including the director as an employee), register with the **Department of Social Insurance Services**. Employer contributions are required. [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm current employer social insurance rates]

---

### Step 9: Appoint an auditor and set up accounting records

Cyprus companies are required to maintain accounting records and file audited financial statements annually (unless exempt). Appoint a Cyprus-registered auditor early. Your accountant will also advise on:
- Corporate tax return deadlines (TD.4)
- Annual return filing with the Registrar (HE32)
- Dividend extraction timing for non-dom purposes

## Common Issues and How to Handle Them

**Issue:** Company name rejected by the Registrar.
**Resolution:** Have your lawyer prepare 2–3 alternative names before filing. The Registrar will reject names already in use, names that are too similar to existing companies, or names with restricted words.

**Issue:** Bank account application delayed or rejected due to KYC documentation gaps.
**Resolution:** Prepare a thorough set of KYC documents from the start — certified passports, current proof of address, source of funds explanation, and a clear business plan. Incomplete packs are the most common cause of delay. Using a well-regarded local lawyer as an introducer to the bank can accelerate the process.

**Issue:** Bank asks for a business plan but the business is early-stage.
**Resolution:** Prepare a 1–2 page description of the business activity, expected clients or revenue sources, and estimated transaction volumes. It does not need to be a formal plan — it needs to demonstrate legitimate business purpose.

**Issue:** Non-EU shareholders or directors face additional KYC requirements.
**Resolution:** Non-EU nationals should expect more extensive due diligence including source of wealth documentation. Factor this into timeline planning.

**Issue:** Company registered but no substance — risk of being treated as tax resident elsewhere.
**Resolution:** Ensure board meetings are held in Cyprus, minutes are recorded, and at least one director is Cyprus-resident or decisions are demonstrably made in Cyprus. Take specialist tax advice on substance requirements before finalising the director structure.

## Field Notes

> **These notes reflect field intelligence from practitioner knowledge as of April 2026. Verify with a qualified Cyprus lawyer and tax adviser before acting.**

**FN-001 — Directorship is the most common way to satisfy the 60-day tax residency business activity condition (April 2026)**
The most widely used method to satisfy the "Cyprus business activity" requirement of the 60-day tax residency rule is for the client to hold a directorship in their Cyprus company. The company does not need to be actively trading in Cyprus — the directorship itself typically satisfies the condition. A tax specialist should confirm this is structured correctly for the client's specific circumstances.
*Source: Practitioner knowledge, April 2026. Cross-referenced with PROC-TAX-PROC-001.*

**FN-002 — Bank account opening is the main bottleneck (April 2026)**
Multiple practitioners consistently report that the bank account is the single most time-consuming step in the company formation process. Hellenic Bank and Bank of Cyprus are the most commonly used. Clients should be told upfront to expect 2–3 months for a full bank account from the day they instruct the lawyer, not 5–10 days.
*Source: Practitioner knowledge, April 2026. Confidence: high.*

**FN-003 — Local nominee director — substance vs. cost tradeoff (April 2026)**
Some service providers offer nominee director services (a local Cyprus-resident director) to satisfy tax substance requirements. This adds annual cost (typically €1,000–€3,000/year) and introduces complexity around liability and control. Clients should understand what they are buying and ensure proper contractual protections are in place. For most owner-managed companies where the owner is also Cyprus tax resident, a nominee director is unnecessary.
*Source: Practitioner knowledge, April 2026. Confidence: medium.*

**FN-004 — EMI accounts as interim solution (April 2026)**
Wise Business and Revolut Business accounts are being used by some clients as interim solutions while waiting for a full Cyprus bank account. These are not bank accounts and do not provide the same credibility or features, but they allow the company to start operating (receiving payments, paying suppliers) while the bank account application progresses. Confirm acceptability with your accountant and any counterparties before relying on an EMI account.
*Source: Practitioner knowledge, April 2026. Confidence: medium.*
