## Summary

In a medical emergency in Cyprus, call **112** (European emergency number — works on all phones including without a SIM). Ambulance, fire, and police all respond to 112. Cyprus public hospitals have 24/7 A&E departments; Paphos General Hospital is the main facility for the Paphos district. GeSY covers emergency treatment for all registered beneficiaries without a GP referral. Non-registered individuals receive emergency treatment but will be billed. Private hospitals also have emergency facilities but at significantly higher cost. Every new resident should know their nearest public hospital and have their insurance documentation accessible before an emergency arises.

## Using A&E at a Public Hospital

1. **Go directly to A&E** — no GP referral required for emergencies
2. **Present at reception** — bring your GeSY card or beneficiary number, passport, and residency document if available
3. **Triage** — a nurse will assess urgency; life-threatening cases are seen immediately; non-urgent cases wait in queue
4. **Treatment** — delivered in Greek and English in most cases at Paphos General
5. **Discharge and follow-up** — receive documentation; follow-up care booked through your Personal Doctor or as directed by the treating doctor

**GeSY coverage at A&E:** Emergency treatment is covered for all registered GeSY beneficiaries. There is a co-payment per emergency visit [NEEDS VALIDATION — confirm amount]. Non-registered individuals are entitled to emergency treatment but will receive a bill.

---

## Using A&E at a Private Hospital

1. **Go to the emergency entrance** — available 24 hours at private hospitals
2. **Present insurance documentation** — have your private health insurance card or policy number; staff will contact insurer if needed
3. **Co-payment** — your insurer may require a co-payment or excess payment upfront; confirm with your policy before an emergency arises
4. **Pre-authorisation** — some insurers require pre-authorisation for non-life-threatening admissions; in a genuine emergency this can be done post-treatment

## Urgent but Non-Emergency Care

For medical needs that are urgent but not life-threatening (high fever, minor injury, out-of-hours illness):

| Option | Details |
|---|---|
| Private GP / walk-in clinic | Available in Paphos — faster than A&E for minor issues; private cost €40–€80 |
| GeSY Personal Doctor | Book urgent appointment via GeSY portal or phone; same-day slots sometimes available |
| Night-duty doctor | District health services run a night-duty roster — [NEEDS VALIDATION — confirm Paphos arrangement] |
| Pharmacist | Cyprus pharmacists are well-qualified and can advise on minor ailments and medications; locate a 24-hour pharmacy via the Cyprus Pharmacy Association website |

**24-hour pharmacies:** Cyprus pharmacies rotate 24-hour duty. The duty pharmacy list is posted at all pharmacy doors and available online. [NEEDS VALIDATION — confirm current website for duty pharmacy lookup in Paphos]

## Notes for Agents

- Advise all clients to identify Paphos General Hospital and at least one private hospital location in their first week. This is basic settling-in advice that prevents panic in an actual emergency.
- Non-GeSY-registered individuals (those who have just arrived and not yet completed PROC-SET-003) will be treated in an emergency but will receive a bill. Advise them to prioritise GeSY registration in the first month.
- The 112 number is unknown to many UK and non-EU clients who default to 999 or their home country's number. Flag this explicitly.
- Private insurance holders: most insurers have a 24-hour emergency line — advise clients to save that number alongside 112 before an emergency arises.
