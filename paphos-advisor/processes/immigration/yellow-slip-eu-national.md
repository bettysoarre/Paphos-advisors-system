## Summary

The MEU1 Registration Certificate — informally known as the Yellow Slip — is the official document that confirms an EU, EEA, or Swiss national's right to reside in Cyprus. Any EU citizen intending to stay in Cyprus for more than three months is legally required to obtain one. The application is submitted to the local Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD) office and results in a registration certificate that does not expire. It is the first and most fundamental document an EU national must obtain when relocating to Cyprus, and it underpins access to tax residency, banking, healthcare registration, and vehicle re-registration.

---

## Who This Applies To

The MEU1 applies to citizens of EU member states, EEA countries (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein), and Switzerland. You are eligible if:

- You are an EU/EEA/Swiss national (not a Cypriot citizen — Cypriots do not need this)
- You intend to stay in Cyprus for more than 3 months
- You fall into one of the following categories:

**Employed or self-employed in Cyprus**
- Must provide proof of employment or self-employment activity in Cyprus

**Financially self-sufficient (not working)**
- Must demonstrate sufficient financial resources to support yourself without becoming a burden on the Cypriot social assistance system
- Must hold comprehensive health insurance valid in Cyprus

**Student**
- Must be enrolled at a recognised educational institution in Cyprus
- Must demonstrate sufficient financial resources and hold health insurance

**Family member of an EU national already registered in Cyprus**
- EU national family members use MEU1; non-EU family members use MEU2 (separate process)

**Edge cases to note:**
- Dual nationals (EU + non-EU) should apply on the basis of their EU nationality
- Retired EU nationals with pension income generally fall under the self-sufficient category
- Freelancers and remote workers working for non-Cypriot clients may need additional documentation to establish their financial basis

## What You Will Need

### Documents Required

Documents required vary slightly by category. The following applies to the most common case — financially self-sufficient or employed applicants.

| Document | Format | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Valid EU passport or national ID card | Original + photocopy | Must be valid at time of application |
| Completed MEU1 application form | Original | Obtainable at the CRMD office or online |
| 2 recent passport-sized photographs | Original | Standard biometric photo requirements |
| Proof of address in Cyprus | Original + photocopy | See notes below |
| Proof of financial resources or employment | Original + photocopy | See notes below |
| Comprehensive health insurance | Original + photocopy | Must cover Cyprus; GESY registration may substitute once registered |
| Payment of €20 fee | Cash or as directed | Paid at point of submission |

**Proof of address — detail:**
- A rental agreement is the most common document used
- The rental agreement must be for a minimum of 12 months
- It must be stamped at the Cyprus Tax Department (Tax Office) — an unstamped rental agreement is typically rejected
- A utility bill in the applicant's name may also be accepted; confirm with the local office

**Proof of financial resources — by category:**
- *Employed:* Employment contract or letter from employer confirming employment, plus recent payslips
- *Self-employed:* Certificate of registration with the Registrar of Companies or Tax Department, plus evidence of business activity
- *Self-sufficient:* Bank statements covering the last 6 months showing both income received and expenditure in Cyprus; there is no officially published minimum threshold but €1,000–€1,500/month net is commonly cited by practitioners as a practical benchmark
- *Student:* Enrolment letter from institution plus evidence of financial support

### Where to Apply

Applications are submitted in person at the **Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD)** district office corresponding to the applicant's place of residence.

| District | Office Location | Notes |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| Paphos | CRMD Paphos District Office, Paphos | Confirm current address with CRMD website |
| Limassol | CRMD Limassol District Office, Limassol | |
| Nicosia | CRMD Nicosia District Office, Nicosia | |
| Larnaca | CRMD Larnaca District Office, Larnaca | |
| Famagusta | CRMD Famagusta District Office | |

**Official portal:** [https://www.gov.cy/moi/en/residence-cards/](https://www.gov.cy/moi/en/residence-cards/)

- The applicant must attend in person — a representative cannot submit on your behalf for initial registration
- Appointments may be required at busier offices; check the CRMD website or call ahead
- Arrive early; offices can be busy, especially in Limassol and Paphos

## Step-by-Step Process

1. **Download or collect the MEU1 form.** Available at the CRMD office or via the official Cyprus government portal. Complete it in full before attending — do not leave fields blank.

2. **Prepare all supporting documents.** Gather all required documents per your category (see Required Documents above). Ensure your rental agreement has been stamped at the Tax Office before attending. Make photocopies of all originals.

3. **Have your rental agreement stamped at the Tax Office.** If using a rental agreement as proof of address, take the original and a copy to your local Tax Department office and have it stamped. This is a separate step that must be completed before the CRMD visit.

4. **Book an appointment if required.** Check whether your district CRMD office requires an appointment. Paphos and Limassol offices are busier — calling ahead is advisable.

5. **Attend the CRMD district office in person.** Bring originals and photocopies of all documents, the completed MEU1 form, 2 passport photos, and €20 in cash.

6. **Submit your application at the desk.** The officer will review your documents. If everything is in order, your application will be accepted. You will pay the €20 fee at this point.

7. **Receive your Yellow Slip.** In some cases the certificate is issued on the same day or within a few days. In practice — particularly in Paphos — you may be given a receipt and told to return or collect by post. See Processing Time below.

### Timeline

**Official stated timeframe:** Issued immediately or within 5 days at the local Immigration City Unit.

**Real-world timeframe (as of early 2026):**
- Nicosia and Larnaca: generally 2–4 weeks
- Limassol: 4–8 weeks
- Paphos: 1–3 months (field intelligence — see Field Notes)

**Factors that cause delays:**
- Incomplete or incorrectly prepared documents at first visit
- Rental agreement not stamped at Tax Office
- Peak periods (summer months, September–October when new arrivals surge)
- Understaffing at district offices, particularly Paphos
- Requests for additional verification of financial resources

**What you receive:** A paper certificate — the Yellow Slip — bearing your name, nationality, address, and registration date. It does not expire and does not need to be renewed. Keep the original safe; replacements require a new application.

## Field Notes

> **These notes reflect field intelligence from practitioner sources and reported real-world experience. They are not official guidance and may vary by officer, office, and timing. Each note should be independently verified.**

**FN-001 — Paphos office processing delays (April 2026)**
The Paphos CRMD office is consistently reported by practitioners as the slowest district for MEU1 processing, with real-world wait times of 1–3 months as of early 2026. The officially quoted same-day or 5-day timeframe does not reflect current Paphos reality. Applicants with time-sensitive banking or tax residency requirements should factor this in and may consider using a government liaison to track progress.
*Source: Multiple law firm practitioner guides, April 2026. Needs validation from a Paphos-based immigration lawyer.*

**FN-002 — Rental agreement stamping is frequently missed**
The requirement to have the rental agreement stamped at the Tax Office before submitting is not prominently stated in all official guidance but is a common rejection trigger at the CRMD desk. Applicants arriving with an unstamped rental agreement will be turned away and must repeat the process.
*Source: Practitioner guides from Philippou Law and Koufettas Law, April 2026.*

**FN-003 — No officially published minimum income threshold**
There is no figure published by CRMD for the minimum financial resources required under the self-sufficient category. Practitioners consistently cite €1,000–€1,500/month net as a practical benchmark that officers appear to accept, but this is not a legal minimum and may vary by officer and circumstance.
*Source: Multiple law firm guides, April 2026. Needs official verification.*

**FN-004 — Utility bills as proof of address**
Some applicants have successfully used a utility bill in their name as proof of address in place of a rental agreement, particularly when staying with family or in owner-occupied property. Acceptance varies by officer and office. If using a utility bill, confirm acceptability with the local CRMD office before attending.
*Source: Community reports and practitioner guidance, April 2026.*

**FN-005 — GESY and health insurance**
Some practitioners report that proof of GESY registration (the General Healthcare System) is being accepted as the health insurance requirement for applicants who are employed in Cyprus and contributing to GESY. For self-sufficient applicants not yet registered with GESY, private health insurance is required.
*Source: Practitioner guidance, April 2026. Needs validation.*

## Common Issues and How to Handle Them

**Issue:** Rental agreement rejected because it has not been stamped at the Tax Office.
**Resolution:** Before attending CRMD, take the rental agreement and a copy to the local Tax Department office and have it stamped. This is a separate step many applicants miss.

**Issue:** Bank statements rejected as insufficient proof of financial resources.
**Resolution:** Ensure statements cover the full 6 months prior to application, show both income and expenditure, and are in English or accompanied by a certified Greek translation. Some officers request an official bank letter in addition to statements.

**Issue:** Application rejected because the rental agreement is for less than 12 months.
**Resolution:** The rental agreement must be for a minimum of 12 months. Short-term or holiday rental agreements are not accepted. If renting, ensure your contract reflects a 12-month term before applying.

**Issue:** Applicant sent away for missing documents without clear explanation.
**Resolution:** Prepare a checklist of all required documents before attending and confirm the list with the local CRMD office or a lawyer in advance. Bringing slightly more documentation than required is better than arriving with gaps.

**Issue:** Certificate not issued on the day — applicant given a receipt and told to wait.
**Resolution:** This is normal, particularly in Paphos and Limassol. Keep your receipt. Follow up after the expected processing period has elapsed. A government liaison or lawyer can chase progress on your behalf.

## Related Processes

- **PROC-TAX-001 — 60-Day Tax Residency Rule:** Many EU nationals obtaining the Yellow Slip are simultaneously establishing Cyprus tax residency. The MEU1 is not required for tax residency but is strong supporting evidence of physical presence. These two processes often run in parallel.
- **PROC-IMM-002 — MEU2 Registration (Non-EU Family Members):** If the applicant has non-EU family members accompanying them, those family members require a separate MEU2 application. The EU national's MEU1 must typically be completed first.
- **PROC-HLTH-001 — GESY Registration:** Healthcare registration typically follows MEU1 completion. The Yellow Slip may be required as part of the GESY registration process.
- **PROC-BIZ-001 — Company Formation:** EU nationals establishing a company in Cyprus will need the Yellow Slip as part of the company formation and bank account opening process.
