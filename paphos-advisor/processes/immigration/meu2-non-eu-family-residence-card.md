## Summary

The MEU2 Residence Card is the official Cyprus residence permit for non-EU nationals who are family members of EU nationals already registered as residents in Cyprus. It grants the holder the right to live and work in Cyprus indefinitely, subject to renewal every five years. It is the primary route for non-EU spouses, civil partners, dependent children, and dependent parents joining an EU citizen who holds a valid MEU1 registration certificate.

---

## Who This Applies To

The MEU2 applies to non-EU, non-EEA nationals who are family members of an EU, EEA, or Swiss national with a valid MEU1 (Yellow Slip) in Cyprus. The qualifying categories under Cyprus law (implementing EU Directive 2004/38/EC) are:

**Automatically qualifying:**
- Spouse of the EU national (including marriages conducted abroad if legally recognised)
- Registered civil partner of the EU national

**Qualifying if financially dependent:**
- Children of the EU national or their spouse — under 21 automatically; over 21 if financially dependent
- Direct ascending relatives (parents, grandparents) of the EU national or their spouse — if financially dependent

**Key conditions that must be met on the application date:**
- The EU national must hold a valid, current MEU1 at the time of application
- Both the EU national and the non-EU applicant must be physically present in Cyprus
- The non-EU applicant must apply within 4 months of their date of entry into Cyprus — late applications face a penalty of up to €2,562.90 and may be refused

**Edge cases:**
- Dual nationals (EU + non-EU) should apply via their EU nationality for MEU1, not this route
- Non-EU family members of UK nationals settled in Cyprus under the Withdrawal Agreement (pre-31 December 2020) should confirm whether they qualify under EU law or need separate advice
- Adult children over 21 claiming dependency must provide clear financial evidence — this category is scrutinised closely

## What You Will Need

### Documents Required

All documents issued outside Cyprus must be apostilled by the competent authority in the issuing country (typically the Ministry of Justice or equivalent — not the Cyprus Ministry of Justice). Documents not in English or Greek require certified English translation by a registered professional translator.

| Document | Format | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| MEU2A application form | Original, completed in blue or black ink only | Obtain from CRMD office or official Cyprus government portal; all fields must be completed |
| Non-EU applicant's valid passport | Original (for inspection) + certified copy | Must be valid for the full intended stay; if recently renewed, provide both old and new |
| EU national's valid passport or national ID | Original (for inspection) + certified copy | — |
| EU national's MEU1 Registration Certificate | Original (for inspection) + copy | Must be current and valid at time of application |
| 2 passport-sized photographs | Original | 35mm × 45mm; white or light grey background; taken within 6 months; face occupying 70–80% of height |
| Marriage or civil partnership certificate | Original + apostille (if issued abroad) + certified English translation | If married in Cyprus: certificate from Church of Cyprus or District Registrar; if married abroad: apostille from issuing country + certified translation |
| Birth certificates for dependent children | Original + apostille (if issued abroad) + certified English translation | Must be full long-form certificate showing both parents' names; abbreviated extracts are not accepted |
| Divorce decree or death certificate of former spouse (if applicable) | Original + apostille (if issued abroad) + certified English translation | Required if either party was previously married |
| Proof of accommodation in Cyprus | Original — see notes below | Rental agreement or property title deed (Land Registry certified) |
| Bank statements (6 months) | Original from bank or certified bank printout | Must show regular income and Cyprus-based expenditures; statements with minimal activity will be scrutinised |
| Health insurance certificate | Original | Must cover Plan A: inpatient + outpatient + medical transport; must be current and valid; GESY registration may substitute if employed and contributing — [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm current CRMD acceptance of GESY for MEU2 applicants] |

| Police clearance certificate | Original + apostille (if issued abroad) + certified English translation | Issued within 6 months preceding application; must show no criminal record |
| Employment confirmation (if employed in Cyprus) | Original letter on employer letterhead + Social Insurance registration certificate | Employer must sign Part III of MEU2A form; include recent payslips |
| Financial resources proof (if self-sufficient) | See notes below | Bank statements covering 6 months; pension statements; dividend certificates; etc. |
| Employer declaration by EU national (if applicable) | As directed by CRMD | Declaration of financial responsibility for dependent applicant |

**Proof of accommodation — detail:**
- Rental agreement is the most common document
- Since late 2024, all rental agreements must be: (a) stamped by the Cyprus Tax Department, AND (b) certified by a Mukhtar (local administrator) or notary public
- Plain signatures without official stamping and Mukhtar/notary certification will result in rejection
- The rental agreement must be for a minimum of 12 months, state the full property address, tenant and landlord names and ID numbers, monthly rent, and rental period
- Original rent payment receipts should accompany the agreement
- If the applicant owns property: a certified Land Registry title deed

**Proof of financial resources — by situation:**
- *Employed in Cyprus:* employer letter, Social Insurance certificate, payslips
- *Self-employed in Cyprus:* company registration certificate, Tax Department registration, 4 months of Social Insurance contributions
- *Employed abroad / remote worker:* employment contract and recent payslips or income statements
- *Self-sufficient / retired:* 6 months of bank statements showing sufficient income; pension letters; investment or interest income statements. ### Where to Apply

Applications are submitted in person at the **Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD)** district office in the district where the applicant lives.

| District | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Paphos | Paphos CRMD District Immigration Unit |
| Limassol | Limassol CRMD District Immigration Unit |
| Nicosia | Central CRMD office — Nicosia; the primary processing centre |
| Larnaca | Larnaca CRMD District Immigration Unit |

**Official portal:** [https://www.moi.gov.cy/en/civil-registry-and-migration/](https://www.moi.gov.cy/en/civil-registry-and-migration/)

- Applications from district offices are forwarded to the central Nicosia CRMD for final decision
- Both the EU national and the non-EU applicant **must attend in person** — a representative cannot substitute

## Step-by-Step Process

**1. Confirm eligibility and the EU national's MEU1 is current.** The EU national's Yellow Slip must be valid at application. If it needs renewal, do that first (see PROC-IMM-001).

**2. Gather and authenticate all documents (4–8 weeks lead time).** Begin apostille and certified translation processes for any foreign-issued documents immediately. UK apostilles through the FCDO and similar authorities typically take 4–6 weeks. Certified translators in Cyprus typically need 2–3 weeks.

**3. Have the rental agreement stamped and certified (if using a rental agreement as proof of address).** Take the rental agreement to your local Cyprus Tax Department office to be stamped for stamp duty, then have it certified by the Mukhtar of your area or a notary public. This is a separate step from signing the rental agreement and must be completed before the CRMD appointment. Arriving without a properly stamped and certified rental agreement results in rejection.

**4. Book an appointment at your CRMD district office.** Aim to book 3–4 weeks in advance. Applications must be submitted within 4 months of the non-EU applicant's date of entry into Cyprus — do not delay.

**5. Conduct a pre-submission document audit.** Verify every document on the list above is present, properly apostilled where required, and correctly translated. Check the MEU2A form is filled completely in blue or black ink. Inconsistencies between the form and supporting documents are a frequent rejection trigger.

**6. Attend the CRMD appointment in person (both applicant and EU national).** Bring originals and certified copies of all documents. The applicant will undergo biometric enrolment (fingerprint and photograph). Pay the €20 fee per applicant.

**7. Receive the submission receipt.** This receipt is proof of pending MEU2 status. The applicant may remain in Cyprus and travel internationally during the processing period using this receipt. Keep it safe — it is the primary document confirming legal status until the card is issued.

**8. Wait for a decision letter.** The official processing period is 6 months. Real-world times in 2025–2026 are typically 6–12 months, with cases extending to 10+ months not uncommon. If a Request for Further Information is issued, respond within the specified timeframe (typically 15–30 days).

**9. Collect the MEU2 Residence Card.** When the decision letter arrives, attend the CRMD office in person to collect the card. The biometric card is valid for 5 years from issuance and must be renewed before expiry (standard renewal procedure applies).

### Timeline

**Official stated processing time:** 6 months from date of application submission.

**Real-world processing time (2025–2026):**
- Typical: 6–9 months
- Extended: 10–15 months in backlog-heavy periods
- Requests for Further Information (RFI) add an average of 3 months

**Key deadlines:**
- Non-EU family member must apply within **4 months of entry into Cyprus** — no exceptions under normal circumstances
- EU national's MEU1 must be valid on application date

⚠ Verify current processing times with the district office or a practitioner before making time-sensitive plans.

## Field Notes

> **These notes reflect field intelligence from practitioner sources and reported real-world experience. They are not official guidance and may vary by officer, office, and timing. Each note should be independently verified.**

**FN-001 — Rental agreement stamping is a frequent rejection trigger (2026)**
Since late 2024, the CRMD strictly enforces the requirement for rental agreements to be stamped at the Tax Department and certified by a Mukhtar or notary. This requirement is not always prominently communicated by landlords or letting agents. Applicants frequently arrive with unsigned or only-landlord-signed agreements and are turned away. Complete this step before attending the appointment.
*Source: Multiple practitioner guides, 2026.*

**FN-002 — Bank statement scrutiny has intensified (2026)**
Immigration authorities in 2026 are examining whether bank statements reflect actual Cyprus-based living (regular local expenditures including grocery shops, utilities, medical visits) rather than nominal presence. Statements showing only periodic deposits without regular local transactions are triggering Requests for Further Information. Ensure statements reflect genuine daily life in Cyprus.
*Source: CX Financia practitioner guide, 2026.*

**FN-003 — Processing backlogs reported in 2025–2026**
Multiple sources document MEU2 applications remaining pending for 10–15 months as of late 2025 and early 2026. The CRMD upgraded its case management system in April 2026 which may have caused additional short-term delays during transition.
*Source: Cyprus Mail, October 2025; Visahq.com, April 2026. Needs validation from Paphos-based practitioner.*

**FN-004 — No officially published minimum income threshold**
There is no published minimum income figure for the self-sufficient category. Practitioners consistently cite €1,000–€1,500/month net as a figure officers appear to accept, but this is not a legal minimum. Acceptance varies by officer and circumstance.
*Source: Multiple practitioner guides, 2026. [NEEDS VALIDATION: Confirm with immigration lawyer whether any threshold has been formalised]*

**FN-005 — Physical presence of EU national is critical**
The EU national cannot be represented by a lawyer or agent at submission — they must attend in person alongside the non-EU applicant. This is the most frequently missed procedural requirement for international families who have not both yet relocated to Cyprus.
*Source: Multiple practitioner guides, 2026.*

**FN-006 — UK nationals: biometric card conversion deadline August 2026**
UK nationals who obtained paper-format MEU1 or MEU3 certificates before 31 December 2020 must convert to biometric cards by 3 August 2026. Failure to convert renders their residence invalid, which in turn affects any MEU2 applications based on their MEU1 sponsorship.

## Common Issues and How to Handle Them

**Issue:** Rental agreement rejected — not stamped by Tax Department or not certified by Mukhtar/notary.
**Resolution:** Before attending the CRMD appointment, take the rental agreement to the local Tax Department office for stamp duty, then to the area Mukhtar or a notary public for certification. Both steps are required.

**Issue:** Bank statements rejected — insufficient evidence of Cyprus-based living.
**Resolution:** Ensure statements cover 6 full months and show regular local expenditures. If statements are from a foreign bank account, ensure they are official originals and show Cyprus-based transactions where possible.

**Issue:** Application refused because the 4-month application window was missed.
**Resolution:** The 4-month window from date of entry is strict. Apply as soon as the EU national's MEU1 is confirmed and accommodation is secured — do not wait until all documents are perfect; submit what you have and follow up with outstanding documents if given a reasonable opportunity.

**Issue:** EU national's MEU1 expired before MEU2 application submitted.
**Resolution:** Renew the MEU1 first (PROC-IMM-001). The MEU2 application cannot proceed to approval if the EU national's MEU1 is not current.

**Issue:** Application pending for more than 9 months with no communication.
**Resolution:** Contact the CRMD district office or engage a government liaison or lawyer to chase status. The April 2026 system upgrade introduced online file tracking — [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm whether this is live for MEU2 applications] — which may allow status checks without in-person visits.

## Related Processes

- **PROC-IMM-001 — Yellow Slip Application (MEU1):** The EU national's MEU1 must be in place before the MEU2 application can be submitted. If the EU national has not yet obtained their Yellow Slip, that process must be completed first.
- **PROC-IMM-003 — MEU3 Permanent Residency Certificate (EU Nationals):** After 5 years of continuous legal residence in Cyprus, EU nationals may upgrade from MEU1 to MEU3 permanent residency status.
- **PROC-HLTH-001 — GESY Registration:** Health insurance is required at MEU2 application. GESY registration typically follows residence card issuance for employed applicants. For self-sufficient applicants, private health insurance must remain in force until GESY is accessible.
