## Summary

Cyprus Permanent Residency by Investment under Regulation 6(2) of the Aliens and Immigration Regulations is a fast-track route to permanent residence for non-EU nationals who make a qualifying investment of at least €300,000 in Cyprus and demonstrate a stable annual income from outside Cyprus. The residence permit is permanent — it does not expire — though the biometric card must be renewed every 10 years. Processing typically takes 2–3 months from a complete application. The holder must visit Cyprus at least once every 2 years to maintain the permit. This route does not grant the right to employment in Cyprus but permits company directorships and dividend income. As of 3 March 2026, the transitional grace period under pre-2023 criteria has ended — all applications now fall under the current, stricter investment and due diligence standards.

---

## Who This Applies To

This route is open **exclusively to non-EU nationals** (EU nationals and EEA/Swiss nationals use the MEU1/MEU3 routes instead).

**Eligibility conditions — all must be met:**
1. Non-EU national
2. Minimum investment of **€300,000** in a qualifying investment category (see Investment Types below)
3. Minimum annual income of **€50,000** from sources outside Cyprus
4. Clean criminal record (applicant and spouse)
5. Not on any EU sanctions list or subject to entry bans to EU or UK
6. No intention to undertake salaried employment in Cyprus

**Income requirement for families:**
- Spouse or civil partner: add **€15,000/year**
- Each dependent minor child: add **€10,000/year**
- Adult children (18–25, full-time students) and dependent parents: [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm exact additional investment/income requirements for adult children and parents — these categories have specific requirements that vary and are not consistently reported in secondary sources]

**Important 2026 update:**

All applications submitted after this date must comply with the current regulatory requirements, including enhanced due diligence standards. ## What You Will Need

### Investment Types

The €300,000 minimum investment must fall into one of the following four categories:

**Category A — Residential real estate (new first sale only)**
Purchase of one or more residential properties from a licensed Cyprus developer through a **first-time sale only** (resale / secondary market properties do not qualify for this category). The combined purchase price must be at least €300,000 + VAT (typically 19%, reduced to 5% in certain circumstances for primary residences). At least €200,000 of the purchase price (+ applicable VAT) must be paid to the seller before the application is submitted. The property must be registered with the Land Registry and the sales agreement stamped within 1 month of signing.

**Category B — Commercial real estate (first sale or resale)**
Purchase of commercial property (offices, retail, hotels, warehouses, or combinations) worth at least €300,000 in total. Unlike Category A, the secondary market qualifies. If the investment is commercial property, the applicant must separately provide proof of residential accommodation in Cyprus (no minimum value required for this accommodation).

**Category C — Cyprus company share capital**
Investment of at least €300,000 in share capital of a Cyprus-registered company that: (a) maintains physical presence in Cyprus; (b) actively conducts business operations; and (c) employs a minimum of 5 full-time Cypriot or EU citizens. Applicant or spouse may serve as unpaid director and receive dividend income.

**Category D — Cyprus investment fund units**
Investment of at least €300,000 in units of a Cyprus-regulated Alternative Investment Fund (AIF), Alternative Investment Fund with Limited Number of Persons (AIFLNP), or Registered Alternative Investment Fund (RAIF) licensed by the Cyprus Securities and Exchange Commission (CySEC), investing solely in Cyprus-based assets.

All documents not in English or Greek require certified professional translation. All foreign-issued documents require apostille from the competent authority in the issuing country.

**Investment-specific documentation — Category A (residential property):**
- Signed and stamped purchase agreement from developer
- Proof of payment (bank transfer records confirming at least €200,000 + VAT paid from foreign account)
- Land Registry contract submission confirmation
- Developer's authorised company details and development licence

**Investment-specific documentation — Category C (company shares):**
- Share purchase or subscription agreement
- Cyprus company registration certificate
- Proof of company operational status (employee payroll records, business activity evidence)
- Evidence of €300,000 capital contribution

### Where to Apply

Applications are submitted to the **Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD)**, primary office in Nicosia. District offices may also accept applications.

**Official portal:** [https://www.moi.gov.cy/en/civil-registry-and-migration/](https://www.moi.gov.cy/en/civil-registry-and-migration/)

**Key difference from other routes:** An authorised legal representative may submit the application on the applicant's behalf (via power of attorney) without the applicant being physically present in Cyprus at that time. However, the applicant must travel to Cyprus for biometric data collection after the application is approved.

## Step-by-Step Process

**1. Select and execute the qualifying investment.** Choose the investment category, identify the specific property/company/fund, and complete the investment transaction. For Category A property, ensure at least €200,000 + VAT is paid to the developer from a foreign bank account before submitting the application. Have the purchase agreement stamped at the Tax Office within 1 month of signing.

**2. Establish the required annual income from outside Cyprus.** Gather 6–12 months of supporting financial documentation demonstrating stable income meeting the €50,000/year threshold (plus increments for family members). If income derives from multiple sources, collect documentation for each source.

**3. Compile the full application package.** Assemble all required documents. Obtain apostilles for foreign documents and arrange certified translations. Police clearance certificates must be recent (within 6 months). Allow 4–6 weeks for overseas apostilles and translations.

**4. Prepare source-of-funds documentation.** Document the legal origin of the €300,000 investment capital. This is subject to enhanced due diligence scrutiny under 2023–2026 regulatory standards. Funds must demonstrably originate from outside Cyprus and be transferred through verifiable banking channels.

**5. Engage a Cyprus immigration lawyer.** While applications may be submitted without legal representation, professional legal assistance is strongly recommended for this route given the complexity of due diligence requirements, the non-refundable €500 application fee, and the scale of the investment at stake.

**6. Submit the application.** The applicant or their authorised legal representative submits the complete application at the CRMD (Nicosia or relevant district office). Receive the submission receipt.

**7. Wait for the due diligence and processing period.** The CRMD will conduct administrative review (2–4 weeks), due diligence assessment (source of funds, investment verification, background screening), and issue a decision. Official timeline: 2–3 months. Complex cases or those requiring extended background checks may take longer.

**8. Receive the approval letter.** On approval, the CRMD issues a formal approval letter. The applicant must travel to Cyprus within 12 months of approval to complete biometric enrolment — if this deadline is missed, the permit lapses automatically.

**9. Attend Cyprus for biometric data collection.** Each approved applicant (including family members) must appear in person at the CRMD for fingerprinting and photograph. This is mandatory — no proxy or remote alternative.

**10. Receive the Permanent Residence Card.** The biometric card is typically issued 2–4 weeks after the biometric appointment. It is mailed to the applicant's registered Cyprus address or held for collection. The card is valid for 10 years (administrative renewal only thereafter — the underlying permanent residence status is indefinite).

---

### Timeline

**Application to decision:** 2–3 months (complete applications under standard due diligence); up to 6 months for complex cases.

**Biometric appointment:** Must occur within 12 months of the approval letter.

**Card issuance:** 2–4 weeks after biometric appointment.

**Card renewal:** Every 10 years (administrative formality; the underlying permanent residence right does not expire).

⚠ Verify current processing times — enhanced due diligence post-March 2026 may affect timelines for new applications.

## Maintaining the Permit After Issue

The permit is permanent but subject to these ongoing conditions:

1. **Invest for the long term:** The qualifying investment must be maintained for as long as the applicant wishes to retain the permit. Selling the property (Category A) without immediately replacing it with another qualifying property of equal or greater value results in automatic permit cancellation. [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm exact replacement timescales and notification requirements with CRMD or lawyer]

2. **Visit Cyprus at least once every 2 years:** The permit is cancelled automatically if the applicant remains absent from Cyprus for more than 2 consecutive years.

3. **Annual confirmation of investment and insurance:** Each year, the applicant must confirm to the CRMD that the qualifying investment is maintained and that valid health insurance remains in force.

4. **Clean criminal record — every 3 years:** Every 3 years, all family members over 18 must submit updated police clearance certificates.

5. **No employment:** Permanent residency holders must not take up salaried employment in Cyprus. Company directorships, shareholdings, and dividend income are permitted.

**Path to citizenship:** After 8 years of continuous legal residence in Cyprus (with the permit maintained throughout), holders may apply for Cyprus citizenship by naturalisation. At citizenship, the investment requirement terminates and the property may be sold. ## Field Notes

> **These notes reflect field intelligence from practitioner sources and reported real-world experience. They are not official guidance and may vary by officer, office, and timing. Each note should be independently verified.**

**FN-001 — Post-March 2026 due diligence is significantly more stringent (2026)**
The March 2026 closure of the transitional grace period means all new applications face the enhanced due diligence framework introduced in 2023. Source-of-funds scrutiny is substantially deeper than before 2023. Applicants with complex wealth structures, multiple business interests, or funds originating from jurisdictions with elevated financial crime risk should expect detailed scrutiny and engage legal counsel experienced in post-2023 due diligence requirements.
*Source: VIsaHQ News, March 2026.*

**FN-002 — Category A property: first-time sale only (2026)**
A common mistake is purchasing a resale residential property and expecting it to qualify for Category A. The resale market does not qualify. The property must be a first-time sale directly from a licensed developer. Applicants should confirm this explicitly with the developer and have their lawyer verify the property's first-sale status before signing.
*Source: Multiple practitioner sources, 2026.*

**FN-003 — VAT reduction for primary residence (5% vs 19%)**
Residential properties intended as the applicant's primary residence may qualify for a reduced VAT rate of 5% rather than the standard 19%. This requires that the total transaction value does not exceed €475,000 and the buildable area does not exceed 190 square metres. Qualifying significantly reduces the overall cost. Applicants should have their lawyer verify eligibility before completing the purchase.
*Source: Multiple practitioner sources, 2026. [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm current VAT reduction eligibility criteria — these rules have been amended historically]*

**FN-004 — 2-year absence rule is strictly enforced (2026)**
The CRMD monitors entry and exit through automated border control systems. Applicants who stay abroad for more than 2 consecutive years will have their permits cancelled automatically. This affects investors who treat Cyprus as a secondary or occasional base — they must plan at least one visit every 2 years.
*Source: Multiple practitioner sources, 2026.*

**FN-005 — Investment PR is not citizenship (2026)**
Some clients conflate this route with the previous Cyprus Citizenship by Investment programme, which was terminated in 2020. The Regulation 6(2) route grants permanent residence, not citizenship. Citizenship requires a separate naturalisation application after 8 years.
*Source: Standard practitioner guidance, 2026.*

## Common Issues and How to Handle Them

**Issue:** Residential property purchased on the secondary market — application rejected because it does not qualify for Category A.
**Resolution:** Only new first-time sales from developers qualify for Category A. Secondary market purchases may qualify under Category B (commercial) if they are commercial properties. Residential resale properties do not qualify for any investment category under current rules. Verify the property's eligibility before signing.

**Issue:** Application rejected due to insufficient source-of-funds documentation.
**Resolution:** Under post-2023 rules, source of funds is scrutinised closely. Prepare comprehensive documentation showing the legal origin and transfer history of investment capital. Engage an experienced immigration lawyer with post-2023 due diligence expertise. Consider allowing 2–3 months for document preparation.

**Issue:** Property sold after permit issued without replacement — permit cancelled.
**Resolution:** If you wish to sell and maintain your permit, purchase a replacement qualifying property of equal or higher value immediately and notify the CRMD. The replacement must meet all original qualifying criteria including the new-first-sale requirement for residential property.

**Issue:** Biometric appointment not attended within 12 months of approval.
**Resolution:** The permit lapses if the biometric appointment is not completed within 12 months of the approval letter. Contact the CRMD before the deadline if travel is proving difficult. Do not allow this deadline to pass without either attending or seeking an extension.

## Related Processes

- **PROC-PROP-001 — Buying a Property:** The Category A (residential) investment route links directly to the property purchase process. A property purchase for PR purposes requires additional steps beyond a standard purchase (payment from foreign account, specific timing of instalment payments, Land Registry registration confirming the investment basis).
- **PROC-TAX-001 — 60-Day Tax Residency Rule:** Investment PR holders who spend 60+ days in Cyprus but less than 183 days may qualify for Cyprus tax residency under the 60-day rule. This is a significant planning consideration for holders wishing to establish Cyprus tax residency while spending limited time on the island.
- **PROC-TAX-002 — Non-Dom Status:** Investment PR holders who become Cyprus tax residents may qualify for the 17-year non-dom exemption on passive income (dividends, interest, rental income). This is a major tax planning benefit for the HNI segment — discuss timing with a Cyprus tax adviser.
