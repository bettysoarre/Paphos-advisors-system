## Summary

The student residence permit allows non-EU, non-EEA nationals to live in Cyprus for the duration of full-time study at a recognised Cyprus university or accredited institution. The permit is issued for the length of the programme (typically 1–4 years) and renewed annually. Entry into Cyprus first requires either a visa (for visa-required nationalities) or visa-free entry; the residence permit is then obtained in-country within 8–30 days of arrival. As of March 2026, students can work limited hours in specified sectors (hospitality, agriculture, manufacturing). Student residence years generally do not count toward the 5-year Long-Term Resident qualifying period, but do count toward the 8-year citizenship eligibility window.

---

## Who This Applies To

The student residence permit applies to **non-EU, non-EEA, non-Swiss nationals** who:
- Have been accepted for full-time study at a recognised Cyprus university or accredited educational institution
- Can demonstrate financial capacity to support their studies and living costs
- Have a clean criminal record
- Are over 18 (some programmes allow 16+)

**Recognised institutions include:**
University of Cyprus, Cyprus University of Technology, European University Cyprus, Frederick University, Neapolis University Paphos, University of Nicosia, and other accredited private colleges.

**Not suitable for:**
- Part-time students (full-time enrolment is required)
- Students attending non-accredited or unrecognised programmes
- Persons who primarily intend to work and study as a secondary purpose

## Step-by-Step Process

**Step 1 — Secure university admission**

Obtain a formal offer of admission letter from a recognised Cyprus institution confirming full-time enrolment, programme start date, and expected duration.

**Step 2 — Obtain visa if required**

Check whether your nationality requires a visa to enter Cyprus. If so, apply at the nearest Cyprus embassy or consulate with the admission letter and supporting financial documentation. Processing typically takes 2–4 weeks.

**Step 3 — Arrange health insurance**

Purchase student health insurance before or immediately upon arrival. Insurance must meet the Plan A equivalent standard for immigration purposes. Most Cyprus universities can advise on approved providers.

**Step 4 — Enter Cyprus**

Travel to Cyprus with the admission letter, visa (if applicable), and supporting documents. Keep a copy of all documents accessible.

**Step 5 — Register for residence permit within 8–30 days**

Within 8–30 days of arrival, attend the local CRMD district office with the full document pack. Submit the application and pay any applicable fee.

**Step 6 — Medical examination in Cyprus**

Book and attend a medical examination with a Cyprus-registered doctor (blood tests and chest X-ray). This can be done on arrival in Cyprus rather than before travel.

**Step 7 — Biometric appointment**

Attend a biometric appointment at the CRMD (fingerprints and photograph). This must be done in person in Cyprus.

**Step 8 — Collect residence permit card**

Collect the student residence permit card from the CRMD on completion of processing. The card is valid for the duration of the current academic year or programme, depending on issue type.

**Step 9 — Annual renewal**

Renew the permit annually before expiry using an updated enrolment letter from the university confirming continued full-time student status.

## Maintaining the Permit

- **Full-time enrolment must be maintained throughout.** Dropping below full-time student status can result in permit revocation. Notify the CRMD immediately if programme changes occur.
- **Annual renewal required.** Submit a renewal application each year before the current permit expires, with an updated enrolment confirmation from the institution.
- **Health insurance must remain current.** Renew health insurance annually alongside the permit.
- **Criminal record updates every 3 years.**
- **Absence limits:** Extended absence from Cyprus (particularly over one academic year) may affect permit validity. Check with CRMD before any prolonged travel.

**Part-time work rights (from March 2026):**
Students may work limited hours in specified sectors — hospitality, agriculture, and manufacturing — subject to hourly restrictions. ## Field Notes

- **Institution recognition is critical.** Only admissions from institutions recognised by the Cyprus Agency of Quality Assurance and Accreditation in Higher Education (CYQAA) qualify. Non-accredited institutions do not satisfy the student permit requirements, regardless of what the institution claims.
- **September is the worst time to apply at CRMD.** The annual September intake creates significant queues at CRMD offices across Cyprus. Students arriving in September should apply for their residence permit as early as possible in that window — do not wait until day 25 or 30.
- **Financial documentation credibility matters.** Large one-off deposits immediately before the application can attract scrutiny. The CRMD may request explanation of the source of funds. Established banking history or scholarship letters are cleaner evidence.
- **Student-to-work transition requires advance planning.** The student permit expires on graduation. If the graduate intends to remain in Cyprus, a work permit application (employer-sponsored) must be ready to file before or immediately upon graduation. There is no automatic grace period beyond the post-graduation job-search permit.
- **LTR pathway note.** Student residence years generally do not count toward the 5-year LTR qualifying period under EU Directive 2003/109/EC. A graduate who works for 5 years after graduating can then apply for LTR — meaning the full pathway to permanent status is graduation plus 5 years of employment. ## Common Issues

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Institution not recognised | Verify CYQAA accreditation before accepting any offer. If the institution is not on the list, the student permit will be refused. |
| Permit not obtained within the 30-day window | Contact a lawyer immediately. Overstaying without a valid permit creates an immigration violation on record. |
| Dropping below full-time enrolment | Notify the CRMD proactively and take legal advice. The permit may be revocable but there may be remedies if the change is temporary (e.g. medical leave). |
| Working outside permitted sectors | This is a permit violation. The permit holder may face cancellation and removal. Restrict work strictly to permitted sectors and hours. |
