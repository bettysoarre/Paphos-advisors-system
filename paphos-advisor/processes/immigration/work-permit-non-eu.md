## Summary

The employer-sponsored work permit is the standard immigration route for non-EU nationals who have secured employment with a Cyprus-registered employer. The process is two-stage: the employer must first obtain labour market approval (demonstrating no suitable EU candidate is available), then the employee enters Cyprus on an entry permit and converts it to a full residence and work permit card. Processing is generally predictable — 6–12 weeks total — and there is no significant backlog for this category. As of 2026, salary thresholds have increased following the minimum wage rise to €1,125/month. Foreign interest companies (registered with the Business Facilitation Unit) benefit from a streamlined route that bypasses the labour market test.

---

## Who This Applies To

Work permits are available to **non-EU, non-EEA, non-Swiss nationals** who:
- Have a valid offer of employment from a registered Cyprus employer
- Meet the salary threshold for their role category
- Pass a medical examination and have a clean criminal record
- Are entering Cyprus specifically for the purpose of that employment

**Main categories:**

| Category | Salary Threshold (2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General employment | €2,000–€3,871/month | Varies by role; subject to labour market test |
| Executive director | €3,872/month minimum | |
| Foreign interest company — key personnel | €2,500/month | Labour market test waived |
| Foreign interest company — middle management | €1,936/month | Labour market test waived |
| EU Blue Card (highly skilled) | €43,632/year | Fast-track; enables EU mobility |
| Seasonal worker | Lower thresholds | Limited to specified sectors; 6-year lifetime cap |

**Not suitable for:**
- Remote workers employed by foreign companies (see PROC-IMM-004 — Digital Nomad Visa)
- Financially independent non-workers (see PROC-IMM-006 — Pink Slip, PROC-IMM-007 — Category F)
- Self-employed individuals (separate registration required)

- **Stage 2 (post-arrival):** Employee attends the **CRMD district office** serving their area of residence within **7 days of entering Cyprus**

## Step-by-Step Process

**Stage 1 — Pre-Arrival (Employer Actions)**

**Step 1 — Labour market testing**

The employer applies to the District Labour Office to advertise the position and demonstrate no suitable EU candidate is available. Standard advertising period is approximately 15 working days. Foreign interest companies registered with the Business Facilitation Unit skip this step entirely.

**Step 2 — Obtain stamped employment contract**

Once labour market approval is confirmed, the employment contract is submitted to the Labour Commissioner for stamping. This is a formal registration step and is required before the CRMD application proceeds.

**Step 3 — Submit CRMD application**

The employer submits Forms MGEN2, M58, and M64 together with all Stage 1 documents to the CRMD. On approval, the CRMD issues an **Entry Permit (Form M70)** authorising the employee to enter Cyprus.

**Step 4 — Employee enters Cyprus**

The employee enters Cyprus using the Entry Permit (Form M70) within the permitted timeframe stated on the permit.

**Stage 2 — Post-Arrival (Employee Actions)**

**Step 5 — Register at CRMD within 7 days**

Within 7 days of entering Cyprus, the employee must attend the local CRMD district office with the Stage 2 document pack. Late attendance may invalidate the entry permit.

**Step 6 — Medical examination in Cyprus**

Book and attend a medical examination with a Cyprus-registered doctor (blood tests and chest X-ray). This must be conducted in Cyprus.

**Step 7 — Biometric appointment**

Provide fingerprints and photographs at the CRMD. The residence and work permit card is issued after processing.

**Step 8 — Receive residence and work permit card**

The permit card is collected from the CRMD on completion of processing. It is valid for the duration of the employment contract, up to the maximums per category.

## Maintaining the Permit

- **Renewal:** Employer submits renewal 1+ month before permit expiry with updated employment contract, payslips, and health insurance documentation
- **Employer change:** Permitted up to 3 times with restrictions. After 3 employer changes, the permit holder must leave Cyprus for 2 years before reapplying
- **Employment reporting:** Employers must register new hires with Social Insurance via the ERGANI system within 1 day of commencement; all employment changes must be reported to the CRMD within 7 days
- **Annual compliance:** Employers must submit employment terms through the ERGANI system annually (January–February window); non-compliance fines up to €5,500

**Path to permanent residence:**
After 5 consecutive years of legal work permit residence, the permit holder becomes eligible for **Long-Term Resident status** (PROC-IMM-008), which grants indefinite residence with full employment rights and EU mobility.

## Field Notes

- **Two authorities involved.** The Department of Labour handles the labour market test; the CRMD handles the immigration permit. Employers must coordinate with both. Foreign interest companies bypass the Department of Labour entirely.
- **Foreign interest company status is a significant advantage.** Companies registered with the Business Facilitation Unit (minimum €200,000 paid-up capital, majority non-EU shareholding) can hire non-EU employees without a labour market test. This substantially reduces the timeline and administrative burden. [NEEDS VALIDATION — confirm current BFU registration requirements as of 2026]
- **ERGANI electronic system is mandatory.** All employment registrations and changes must now flow through the ERGANI digital platform. Employers not familiar with ERGANI should work with their accountant or lawyer to ensure compliance from day one.
- **Salary thresholds increased in January 2026.** The national minimum wage rose to €1,125/month in January 2026, and work permit salary thresholds rose proportionally. Confirm current thresholds with a lawyer at point of application — they are subject to annual review.
- **EU Blue Card (from July 2025).** Cyprus implemented the EU Blue Card for highly skilled workers from 7 July 2025. The Blue Card enables EU-wide mobility and has a fixed 90-day processing cap. Qualifying income: approximately €43,632/year. [NEEDS VALIDATION — confirm Blue Card take-up and practical processing times at Cyprus CRMD]

## Common Issues

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Employee does not register at CRMD within 7 days of arrival | This is a serious breach. Contact a lawyer immediately — a legal remedy may be possible but the entry permit may be invalidated. |
| Employer changes employer more than 3 times | The permit holder must leave Cyprus for 2 years before re-applying. Plan career moves carefully. |
| Salary falls below the threshold mid-contract | The employer must rectify immediately. Operating below the salary threshold is a grounds for permit revocation and can expose the employer to significant fines. |
| ERGANI system registration issues | Use the employer's accountant or an HR lawyer to manage ERGANI registration. Delays of even one day constitute a breach. |
