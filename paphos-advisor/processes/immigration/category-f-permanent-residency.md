## Summary

Category F is the standard permanent residence permit for non-EU, non-EEA nationals who are financially independent and do not intend to work in Cyprus. Unlike the Pink Slip (temporary, renewed annually), Category F is permanent — the card is valid for 10 years and renewed indefinitely once granted. The minimum income requirement is €9,568 per year (lower than the Pink Slip's €24,000), but Category F is subject to a severe processing backlog: as of 2026, applications submitted today are unlikely to be processed before 2032–2033. The standard practice is to **apply for the Pink Slip immediately** (6–8 months processing) and **apply for Category F simultaneously**, holding the Pink Slip while Category F processes. Work is prohibited under both routes.

---

## Who This Applies To

Category F is open to **non-EU, non-EEA, non-Swiss nationals** who:
- Are financially self-sufficient with a stable income from sources outside Cyprus
- Do not intend to work or engage in any economic activity in Cyprus
- Are not employees of a Cyprus company or self-employed in Cyprus
- Can demonstrate legal entry into Cyprus and a clean criminal record

**This is the primary long-term permanent route for:**
- UK nationals post-Brexit who are retired or living on investment/pension income
- Non-EU nationals who want permanent rather than temporary status

**Not suitable for:**
- Anyone who intends to work in Cyprus (see PROC-IMM-009 — Work Permit)
- Remote workers earning employment income from a foreign employer (see PROC-IMM-004 — Digital Nomad Visa)
- Investors with €300,000+ capital who want fast-track permanent residency (see PROC-IMM-005 — Regulation 6(2))

## What You Will Need

### Documents

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Valid passport | Minimum 6 months validity from application date |
| Form M.67 / MIP2 | One per family member; obtain from CRMD |
| 12–24 months bank statements | Showing regular deposits from abroad; practitioners recommend demonstrating €20,000–€50,000+ actual annual income despite the €9,568 minimum |
| Income documentation | Pension letters, employment contracts, dividend/investment statements, rental income proof |
| Criminal record certificate | From country of origin and any country of 6+ months residence in past 5 years; within 6 months of application; apostilled |
| Health insurance certificate | Plan A or equivalent private health insurance; GeSY alone not accepted |
| Proof of accommodation | Rental agreement (minimum 12 months) or property title deed |
| Medical examination results | Blood tests and chest X-ray conducted in Cyprus by registered doctor |
| Marriage/birth certificates | For dependents; apostilled and translated |
| Curriculum vitae | |

All foreign documents must be apostilled and translated into Greek or English by a sworn translator.

### Fees

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Government application fee | €500 |
| Residence card fee | €30 per person (at collection) |

## Step-by-Step Process

**Step 1 — Prepare documentation**

Assemble the full document pack. All foreign documents must be apostilled and translated before submission. Allow 4–8 weeks for gathering criminal records, apostilles, and translations.

**Step 2 — Book medical examination in Cyprus**

Book a medical examination with a registered Cyprus doctor for blood tests (HIV, Hepatitis B/C, syphilis, TB) and chest X-ray. This must be conducted in Cyprus, not abroad.

**Step 3 — Submit application to CRMD**

Submit the complete application pack in person at the CRMD, together with Form M.67/MIP2 and the €500 fee. Receive a receipt confirming submission.

**Step 4 — Biometric appointment**

Attend a separate biometric appointment (fingerprints, photograph, signature) at the CRMD. The date is assigned at submission or communicated by letter.

**Step 5 — Wait for processing**

Given the current backlog, this stage is measured in years, not months. As of 2026, the CRMD is processing applications submitted in 2020–2021. Expect 5–7 years or longer. [NEEDS VALIDATION — confirm current approximate processing year with Paphos CRMD as of mid-2026]

**Step 6 — Respond to information requests**

The CRMD may issue requests for additional documentation. Respond promptly — delays in responding can further extend processing time.

**Step 7 — Collect permanent residence card**

On approval, attend the CRMD to collect the residence card. Pay the €30 card fee. The card is valid for 10 years and renewed indefinitely.

## Maintaining the Status

Once granted, Category F permanent residence is maintained by:

- **Visiting Cyprus at least once every 2 years** — absences exceeding 2 consecutive years can result in permit cancellation
- **Renewing the residence card every 10 years** — status does not expire; only the card document requires renewal
- **Maintaining health insurance** — current health insurance must be held throughout
- **Maintaining accommodation** — must have a registered address in Cyprus
- **Criminal record updates** — certificates required at least every 3 years

**Work prohibition remains permanent** — Category F holders may never engage in employment or economic activity in Cyprus, including remote work. This is a lifelong condition of the permit.

## Field Notes

- **The backlog is real and severe.** The February 2026 government audit documented 12,000 pending citizenship and residency applications, some dating to 2007. Do not plan timelines around Category F approval.
- **Pink Slip + Category F is the standard strategy.** Apply for both simultaneously. The Pink Slip gives legal status immediately; Category F converts that to permanent status when eventually approved.
- **Investors should consider Regulation 6(2).** If an applicant has €300,000+ capital available, the investment PR route (PROC-IMM-005) processes in 2–3 months and provides immediate permanent status — a dramatically better outcome than waiting 7 years for Category F.
- **Income threshold mismatch.** Category F requires only €9,568/year, but practitioners consistently recommend demonstrating €20,000–€50,000+ because the CRMD has discretion to question adequacy. The Pink Slip's €24,000 threshold is actually higher, which creates the odd situation that Pink Slip applicants are held to a stricter income test than Category F.
- **Nicosia submission requirement.** [NEEDS VALIDATION] — confirm whether Paphos district applicants must travel to Nicosia for submission or whether Paphos CRMD handles Category F applications locally.

## Common Issues

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| No acknowledgement from CRMD after submission | Request a written confirmation receipt at point of submission. Keep the original stamped copy. |
| Information request received years after application | Respond within the timeframe stated. Use a government liaison to ensure the response is correctly received and logged. |
| Application appears "lost" in the system | A lawyer or government liaison can make a formal status enquiry. This is sometimes the only way to confirm an application is still active. |
| Applicant wishes to work while waiting | Work is prohibited under Category F and the Pink Slip. The only legal pathway to work is to switch to a work permit route, which is incompatible with the financially independent category. |
