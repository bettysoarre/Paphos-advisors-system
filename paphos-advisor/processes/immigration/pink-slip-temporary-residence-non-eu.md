## Summary

The Pink Slip is the informal name for Cyprus's Temporary Residence Permit (Visitor Category) for non-EU nationals who wish to reside in Cyprus for more than 90 days as financially independent individuals — retirees, those living on investment income, or people who have no intention of working in Cyprus. It is valid for one year and renewed annually, subject to the holder continuing to meet income and residence requirements. The minimum annual income requirement is €24,000 from outside Cyprus. Work of any kind is prohibited. This is the **primary immigration route for UK nationals post-Brexit** who are retired or self-sufficient. It is distinct from both Category F (permanent residency, same no-work condition but €9,568/year threshold and currently a 5–7 year processing backlog) and the Digital Nomad Visa (which permits remote work but requires active employment income of €3,500/month).

---

## Who This Applies To

The Pink Slip is open to **non-EU, non-EEA nationals** who:
- Are financially self-sufficient with income from sources outside Cyprus
- Do not intend to work or engage in any economic activity in Cyprus
- Wish to reside in Cyprus for longer than 90 consecutive days

**Primary target groups:**
- UK nationals post-Brexit (no longer EU citizens; Yellow Slip route no longer available for new arrivals)
- US, Canadian, Australian, and other non-EU nationals retiring to Cyprus
- Financially independent individuals (investment income, dividends, interest)
- Individuals awaiting Category F approval (which currently has a 5–7 year processing backlog)

**Not for:**
- EU, EEA, or Swiss nationals — they use the MEU1/MEU2 route
- UK nationals who were already legally resident in Cyprus on or before 31 December 2020 — they have Withdrawal Agreement rights (confirm individually)
- Remote workers wanting to continue working — they should use the Digital Nomad Visa
- Investors making a €300,000 qualifying investment — they should use the Investment PR (PROC-IMM-005)

**Comparison with Category F:**
The Pink Slip and Category F are two separate routes — not the same permit. The critical differences:

| | Pink Slip | Category F |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Temporary (1 year, annually renewable) | Permanent (indefinite) |
| Minimum annual income | €24,000 (single) | €9,568 (single) |
| Processing time | 6–8 months | 5–7 years (current backlog) |
| Government fee | €90 first time; €70 renewal | €500 |
| Work rights | None | None |

The most practical strategy for self-sufficient non-EU nationals is to obtain a Pink Slip for immediate residence AND simultaneously apply for Category F, then continue on Pink Slip while Category F processes over several years.

**ICP relevance:**
- **ICP-001 (UK National Retiree):** the primary and most relevant route for this segment

**Proof of income from abroad — by source:**
- *UK state and occupational pension:* original pension award letter or annual pension statement from DWP / pension provider confirming monthly/annual amount
- *Investment income:* bank statements showing dividend/interest deposits; investment account statements; dividend payment vouchers from companies
- *Rental income from property abroad:* rental agreement(s) for the property + bank statements showing receipt of rental payments
- *Other passive income:* relevant account statements and documentation confirming the income is ongoing and from outside Cyprus

**Proof of accommodation — detail:**
- Rental agreement: minimum 12 months; must be stamped at the Cyprus Tax Department and certified by the Mukhtar of the area or a notary public
- Or: property title deed certified by the Land Registry
- Short-term or holiday rentals are not accepted

## Step-by-Step Process

**1. Confirm eligibility.** Confirm you are a non-EU national, financially self-sufficient, and have no intention of working in Cyprus. If you plan to work remotely, consider the Digital Nomad Visa instead (PROC-IMM-004).

**2. Open a Cyprus bank account and establish a transfer pattern.** The CRMD requires Cyprus bank statements showing at least €2,000/month in deposits transferred from abroad. Open a Cyprus bank account and establish a pattern of regular foreign income transfers as early as possible — ideally 6–12 months before the application if possible. [NEEDS VALIDATION: whether a shorter transfer history (e.g. 3 months) is accepted for first-time applicants]

**3. Secure accommodation and prepare the rental agreement.** Ensure your rental agreement covers at least 12 months, is stamped at the Tax Department, and is certified by your Mukhtar or a notary public.

**4. Gather and authenticate all documents.** Begin this process well in advance. UK police clearance (ACRO certificate), pension letters, bank statements, and apostilled documents all take time. Allow 4–6 weeks for UK apostilles.

**5. Arrange the medical examination.** Book and complete the blood serology tests and chest X-ray at a clinic in Cyprus. Certificates are valid for 3 months from the date of examination — time this so the certificates do not expire before your application is submitted.

**6. Obtain Plan A health insurance.** Purchase a Plan A policy from a Cyprus-registered or accepted insurer before the application date. Confirm it covers inpatient, outpatient, and repatriation costs to the required minimum of €13,669/year.

**7. Book an appointment at your CRMD district office.** Contact the Paphos or relevant district office as early as possible — slots can be weeks out. The application must be submitted before your current legal status in Cyprus (e.g. 90-day tourist entry) expires.

**8. Submit the application in person.** Attend the appointment with all originals and copies. Biometric data (fingerprints and photograph) will be collected. Pay the €90 government fee per applicant.

**9. Receive the submission receipt.** The receipt confirms lawful status in Cyprus during the processing period. Keep it safe — it is the primary proof of legal presence until the permit card is issued.

**10. Wait for a decision.** Processing typically takes 6–8 months. Respond promptly to any requests for further information. During this period, you may remain in Cyprus (and typically re-enter if you travel) using the receipt.

**11. Collect the Pink Slip residence card.** Attend the CRMD office to collect the card when the decision letter arrives. The card is valid for 1 year from issuance.

**12. Renew before expiry, each year.** Submit the renewal application at least 2 months before expiry. Renewals require updated bank statements, renewed health insurance, and an updated police clearance (every 3 years for adults).

---

### Timeline

**Initial application processing time:** 6–8 months typical; 4–12 months range depending on documentation completeness and CRMD workload.

**Validity:** 1 year from date of issue.

**Renewal:** Annual; must apply at least 2 months before expiry. Renewal processing is faster than initial — typically 3–6 months.

⚠ The application must be submitted before your current lawful entry permission (tourist 90-day allowance) expires. Do not let this window close before submitting.

## Maintaining the Permit

- **Do not be absent from Cyprus for more than 3 consecutive months.** Extended absences trigger automatic permit cancellation. Short holidays are fine; extended trips back to the UK should be planned carefully.
- **Maintain your income levels.** Annual bank statements confirming ongoing income meeting the threshold are required for renewal.
- **Keep health insurance current.** Plan A coverage must remain valid throughout each year of the permit.
- **Update police clearance certificates every 3 years** at renewal.
- **Do not work or engage in any economic activity in Cyprus.** Company directorships (unpaid) and dividend income are permitted; salaried employment and active self-employment are not.

**Pathway to longer-term status:**
- After 5 continuous years on a Pink Slip: eligible to apply for **long-term resident status** (EU-wide status; work rights; more stable legal position)
- After 8 continuous years: eligible to apply for **Cyprus citizenship** (requires A2 Greek language proficiency and other integration requirements)
- Simultaneously: apply for **Category F permanent residency** at any point — the application will take 5–7 years to process, but filing early starts the clock

## Field Notes

> **These notes reflect field intelligence from practitioner sources and reported real-world experience. They are not official guidance and may vary by officer, office, and timing. Each note should be independently verified.**

**FN-001 — Pink Slip is distinct from Category F — do not confuse them (2026)**
Despite being described by some sources as "Pink Slip Category F", these are separate permits. The Pink Slip is temporary (annual renewal). Category F is permanent but has a 5–7 year processing backlog as of 2026. The most practical strategy for self-sufficient non-EU nationals is to apply for both simultaneously: Pink Slip for immediate residence while Category F processes over several years.
*Source: GK Law Firm, Koufettas Law, British Expats forum, 2026.*

**FN-002 — UK nationals: this is the primary post-Brexit route for retirees (2026)**
UK nationals who arrived after 31 December 2020 no longer qualify for the EU freedom of movement route (Yellow Slip / MEU1). The Pink Slip is the standard route for UK retirees. The UK state pension income (typically over £10,000/year = ~€11,800) combined with an occupational pension usually meets the €24,000/year threshold. Take specialist tax advice — UK pension income taxed in Cyprus is subject to a favourable 5% flat rate.
*Source: GK Law Firm, Mitos Relocation, 2026.*

**FN-003 — Tax position: UK pension taxed at 5% flat rate in Cyprus (2026)**
UK retirees who become Cyprus tax residents (183+ days per year in Cyprus) can elect to have their UK pension income taxed in Cyprus at 5% flat rate (on amounts above €5,000/year) rather than UK income tax rates. This is a major financial consideration. The UK-Cyprus Double Tax Treaty ensures they will not be taxed on the same income in both countries simultaneously. In addition, Cyprus non-dom status exempts dividend, interest, and inheritance from Cyprus tax for up to 17 years.
*Source: Mitos Relocation, Cyprus Tax Life, 2026. [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm current flat rate pension election is still 5% and that DTA provisions have not changed]*

**FN-004 — Paphos office: longer processing times reported (2026)**
Based on patterns observed for MEU1 applications, the Paphos district office is generally reported as slower than Limassol and Nicosia. Real-world Pink Slip processing times in Paphos are likely at the higher end of the 6–8 month range, and in some cases longer. A government liaison can help track progress.
*Source: Inference from MEU1 field notes; [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm Pink Slip processing times specifically in Paphos from a practitioner or government liaison — Chrystalla can provide this]*

**FN-005 — Bank guarantee exemption for UK nationals is reported but needs verification (2026)**
Some sources report that UK nationals are exempt from the bank guarantee requirement (typically €350–€850 per person). Others do not mention this. This is a minor financial point but worth confirming before attending the appointment.
*Source: LA Law Cyprus guide, November 2025. [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm bank guarantee requirement and UK exemption with CRMD or practitioner]*

**FN-006 — Medical examinations must be done in Cyprus for first-time applications (2026)**
First-time applicants must have the blood serology and chest X-ray examinations performed in Cyprus by licensed medical professionals. Certificates from the applicant's home country are not accepted. This is an additional logistical step that must be scheduled before the appointment — medical certificate validity is only 3 months.
*Source: Multiple practitioner sources, 2026.*

## Common Issues and How to Handle Them

**Issue:** Application submitted too late — tourist 90-day entry permission has expired.
**Resolution:** Overstaying the 90-day entry before submitting the application creates a breach. Submit the application before the 90-day tourist allowance expires. If already overstayed, take legal advice immediately before attending the CRMD — it may be necessary to exit Cyprus and re-enter.

**Issue:** Bank statements do not show 12 months of regular income because the Cyprus account was opened recently.
**Resolution:** Provide statements from the date of account opening, supplemented by additional evidence of income (pension award letters, overseas bank statements, SWIFT transfer records). [NEEDS VALIDATION: confirm with Paphos CRMD what minimum bank history is accepted for first-time UK retiree applications — Chrystalla may know this]

**Issue:** Rental agreement rejected — not stamped or not certified by Mukhtar.
**Resolution:** Same requirement as for MEU1/MEU2. Have the rental agreement stamped at the local Tax Department and certified by the area Mukhtar or a notary public before attending the appointment.

**Issue:** Absent from Cyprus for more than 3 consecutive months — permit cancelled.
**Resolution:** The permit is cancelled automatically. The holder must start the application process again from scratch. Plan travel carefully to avoid triggering the 3-month continuous absence rule.

**Issue:** Income falls below €24,000/year at renewal.
**Resolution:** The renewal may be refused if bank statements show income consistently below the threshold. If income has fallen due to a temporary circumstance, gather supporting documentation explaining the situation. ## Related Processes

- **PROC-TAX-001 — 60-Day Tax Residency Rule:** Pink Slip holders who are present in Cyprus for 60+ days but less than 183 days may be able to establish Cyprus tax residency under the 60-day rule, unlocking non-dom and other tax benefits while spending limited time in Cyprus.
- **PROC-TAX-002 — Non-Dom Status:** Pink Slip holders who become Cyprus tax residents qualify for the 17-year non-dom exemption on dividends, interest, and inherited assets. Of particular relevance to UK retirees with investment portfolios.
- **PROC-IMM-005 — Permanent Residency by Investment (Regulation 6(2)):** An alternative route for non-EU nationals with €300,000 to invest who want permanent residency without the 5–7 year Category F wait. Compared to the Pink Slip: higher upfront cost but permanent status; lower annual income requirement (€50,000/year); no 3-month absence restriction.
