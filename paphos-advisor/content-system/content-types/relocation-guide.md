# Content Type: Relocation Guide

## Definition

A Relocation Guide is the primary long-form content format for Paphos Advisors. Each guide is the definitive, standalone resource on a specific relocation topic, process, or journey stage. It is written for a clearly defined ICP segment and covers the subject comprehensively enough that a reader does not need to go elsewhere to understand the topic.

Guides serve dual purposes: they establish topical authority with search engines, and they build trust with prospective clients who are evaluating whether Paphos Advisors understands their situation.

---

## Purpose

To inform and convert. A Relocation Guide answers a reader's core question in full — leaving them with enough clarity to understand their situation and enough trust to take the next step with Paphos Advisors. The guide is both a search acquisition asset (targeting informational queries) and a qualification tool (readers who engage with a guide are self-identifying as relevant prospects).

---

## When to Use This Type

Use a Relocation Guide when:

- The topic requires more than 500 words to explain properly — a shorter piece would give an incomplete answer
- The reader needs sequential or layered understanding, not just a list of answers
- The topic is a complete process or journey stage (e.g. "Getting a Yellow Slip", "How Tax Residency Works for Remote Workers")
- The goal is both search visibility and trust-building, not just quick answers

**Use a Checklist instead when:** the reader already understands the process and needs a task list to execute it.

**Use an FAQ Page instead when:** the topic is best answered as discrete Q&A pairs and does not require sequential narrative.

**Use a Process Explainer instead when:** the goal is to explain a single concept or mechanism rather than walk through a full journey.

---

## Required Sections

Every Relocation Guide must include all of the following sections, in this order:

1. **Introduction** (100–200 words) — opens by acknowledging the reader's situation and what they are trying to accomplish; states clearly what the guide covers; includes the primary keyword naturally in the first 100 words. Do not begin with "In this guide, we will..." — lead with the reader's context.

2. **What You Need to Know First** (200–400 words) — the single most important thing the reader needs to understand before proceeding; sets expectations on complexity, timeline, and cost; flags any eligibility conditions or prerequisites upfront.

3. **Eligibility / Who This Applies To** (150–300 words) — defines which reader this guide is written for; flags differences by nationality, residency status, or circumstances where relevant.

4. **Step-by-Step Process** (600–1,500 words) — the core instructional content; numbered steps where content is sequential, headed sections where it is not; each step must include what to do, where to go, what documents or costs are involved, and what to expect. Name the department, the form, the fee, the address — vague guidance erodes trust.

5. **Documents and Fees** (200–400 words) — a dedicated scannable section listing every document and fee required; use a table; state original vs. copy, apostille requirements, translation requirements; include the date fees were last confirmed.

6. **Timeline** (100–200 words) — realistic end-to-end timeline; distinguish official stated timeframes from real-world experience; flag district-level variations if they exist.

7. **Common Mistakes / Practical Tips** (200–400 words) — what people commonly get wrong and what practitioners recommend; clearly marked as field experience, not official guidance; at least 3 specific tips.

8. **When to Get Professional Help** (100–150 words) — explicit trigger points where a reader should stop self-managing and engage an advisor or specialist; natural partner referral point.

9. **Related Guides** (100–200 words) — what process or topic typically follows this one; 3–5 internal links to related guides, process documents, or FAQs; ends with the primary CTA.

---

## Optional Sections

Include when relevant to the specific guide:

- **Case example or scenario** — a worked example illustrating the process for a typical client profile (anonymised)
- **Comparison table** — when the guide covers a decision with multiple routes (e.g. 183-day rule vs. 60-day rule)
- **Costs summary box** — a consolidated cost summary when fees are spread across multiple steps
- **Glossary** — for guides that use technical or legal terms the audience may not know
- **FAQ section** — short Q&A block for common follow-on questions; triggers FAQPage schema if included

---

## Word Count Range

| Level | Count |
|-------|-------|
| Minimum | 2,000 words |
| Target | 2,500–3,500 words |
| Maximum | 4,000 words |

Guides below 2,000 words are not comprehensive enough to rank or build authority. Beyond 4,000 words, split into multiple linked guides or a guide with sub-articles. Aim for the word count that fully covers the topic — do not pad to hit a number.

## SEO Requirements

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Primary keyword | Must appear in: H1, first 100 words, at least one H2, meta description |
| Title tag | 50–60 characters; include primary keyword; do not keyword-stuff |
| Meta description | 140–155 characters; include primary keyword; written for clicks |
| URL slug | kebab-case; primary keyword; max 5–6 words; no stop words |
| H1 | One only; matches or closely mirrors title tag |
| H2s | 4–8 per guide; include secondary keywords naturally |
| Internal links | Minimum 3; link to related process docs, guides, and service pages |
| External links | Official sources only (government portals, legislation); open in new tab |
| Schema markup | HowTo schema for step-by-step guides; FAQPage schema if FAQ section is included |
| Image alt text | All images must have descriptive alt text including keyword where natural |

**Keyword research requirement:** Every guide must have a defined primary keyword with a known search volume before briefing begins. Do not write guides without keyword validation.

---

## Source Requirements

| Source Type | Requirement |
|-------------|-------------|
| Official sources | At least one official source (government portal, legislation, regulatory body) cited for any factual claim about process, fees, or eligibility |
| Internal knowledge base | All factual claims must be linked to or derived from a validated KB article (KB-XXX) or process document (PROC-XXX) |
| Field intelligence | Any practitioner notes or field observations must be clearly flagged as such — not presented as official guidance |
| Recency | No source older than 24 months cited without a note that it has been verified as still current |
| Prohibited | Do not cite expat blogs, forums, or community posts as primary sources |

---

## Call to Action

Every Relocation Guide must end with a single, specific CTA matched to where the reader is in their journey. Do not include multiple competing CTAs.

| Guide Type | Primary CTA |
|------------|-------------|
| Process guide (immigration, tax, property) | "Book a free consultation to discuss your specific situation" |
| Settling-in guide | "Download the [relevant] checklist" |
| Comparison guide | "Speak to an advisor to find the right route for you" |
| Lead magnet gateway guide | "Download the complete guide" |

---

## Update Cadence

| Content Volatility | Review Frequency |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| Evergreen (process unlikely to change) | Every 12 months |
| Volatile (fees, quotas, timelines, policy) | Every 3 months |
| Mixed | Every 6 months, or immediately when a linked process document is updated |

**Triggers for immediate review:**
- A linked PROC-XXX document is updated
- A regulatory change is announced in the relevant area
- A client or reader flags incorrect information
- An official source URL changes or returns a 404

**On review, check:** fees and timelines still current; all external links live; all internal links resolve; process matches the current linked PROC-XXX document; title and meta description still accurate.

---

## Quality Checklist

Before a guide is marked as approved for publication:

- [ ] Word count is between 2,000 and 4,000
- [ ] All required sections are present and in order
- [ ] Primary keyword appears in H1, first 100 words, at least one H2, and meta description
- [ ] Title tag is 50–60 characters
- [ ] Meta description is 140–155 characters
- [ ] At least one official source is cited
- [ ] All factual claims are linked to a validated KB or PROC document
- [ ] Field intelligence is clearly marked as such
- [ ] Minimum 3 internal links included
- [ ] CTA is present, specific, and singular
- [ ] Fees include the date last confirmed
- [ ] HowTo or FAQPage schema is specified if applicable
- [ ] Frontmatter is complete including `target_keyword`, `target_icp`, and `related_processes`
