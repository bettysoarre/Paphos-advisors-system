# Accuracy and Disclaimer Rules

## Purpose
Defines the standards for factual accuracy in published content and the rules governing when and how disclaimers are applied.

---

## Accuracy Standards

### The source chain rule
Every factual claim in published content must be traceable through this chain:

```
Published content claim
    ↓ sourced from
Process doc (processes/) or KB article (knowledge/)
    ↓ sourced from
Official source (government website / legislation)
    and/or
Field source (partner interview / practitioner observation)
```

If you cannot trace a claim to this chain, it must not be published.

### No "everyone knows" claims
Statements like "Cyprus is well known for..." or "It is widely understood that..." are not acceptable as factual claims without sources. If it is true, source it. If it cannot be sourced, reframe it as a general observation and do not present it as fact.

### Currency requirement
All factual claims must reflect the current position. When in doubt about currency:
- Check the `updated` date on the process doc
- If the process doc has not been reviewed in more than its review cadence, do not publish content from it until it has been reviewed

### Specific over vague
Do not use vague language where specific facts are known:
✓ "Processing typically takes 1-3 months at the Paphos CRMD office"
✗ "Processing times can vary and may take several months"

If you only know a range, give the range. If you do not know, say you do not know — and why.

## Confidence-Based Content Rules

| Source confidence | What is allowed |
|---|---|
| High | State as fact. Standard disclaimer applies. |
| Medium | State as fact with enhanced accuracy disclaimer. |
| Low | State as field intelligence, not confirmed fact. Enhanced disclaimer required. |
| Unverified | Do not publish until validated. |

---

## Disclaimers (see also `legal-disclaimer-rules.md`)

**Standard disclaimer:** Required on all pages. Footer placement minimum.

**Enhanced accuracy disclaimer:** Required in a callout box when any content on the page is sourced from medium or low confidence material.

**Regulatory change notice:** Required when a process has recently changed and the content has not yet been fully updated.

**Professional advice CTA:** Required on all process-heavy pages. "For advice specific to your situation, speak to a qualified [immigration lawyer / tax advisor]."

## What to Do When You Are Uncertain

**If uncertain about one fact:** Note the uncertainty explicitly ("we are currently verifying this") and run a research session before publishing or updating.

**If a whole section is uncertain:** Do not publish the section until it is validated. Publish what you know; leave the uncertain section out until ready.

**If a regulation has changed but you do not yet have the full picture:** Publish a short update notice ("This area has recently changed. We are updating our guidance. Please verify with an official source.") rather than removing the page or publishing unvalidated new information.

## Reader Complaints About Accuracy

If a reader contacts us to say content is inaccurate:
1. Do not dismiss the complaint — investigate first
2. Check the claim against the relevant process doc and KB articles
3. If they are right: correct the content immediately, thank them, update the source docs
4. If they are wrong: explain the basis for the published information
5. Log the complaint in the Content Pipeline notes for the relevant piece
