# Editorial Standards

## Structure

### Every page must have
- H1 (page title — one only)
- A clear opening paragraph that answers the key question in plain English (before any heading structure)
- Logical H2 hierarchy
- A summary or next steps section at the end

### Header hierarchy
- H1: Page title (one per page)
- H2: Major sections
- H3: Sub-sections within H2s
- H4: Rarely needed. If you are reaching H4, consider whether the content is too nested.

Do not skip levels (H1 → H3 without H2).

---

## Formatting

### Lists
Use bullet lists for unordered items. Use numbered lists for sequential steps. Do not use lists for items that read better as prose.

Minimum 2 items in a list. Maximum ~10 before breaking into sections.

### Tables
Use tables for structured comparisons, document requirements, timelines, and fee summaries. Do not use tables for simple lists.

### Bold
Use for key terms, important warnings, and critical actions. Do not overuse — bold loses its meaning if half the page is bold.

### Italics
Use sparingly — for terms being defined, or for titles of documents. Not for emphasis.

### Callout boxes
Use for:
- Important warnings (⚠ Warning: ...)
- Tips or shortcuts (💡 Tip: ...)
- Last updated notices
- Accuracy disclaimers

---

## Numbers and Dates

- Dates: DD Month YYYY (1 January 2026) in body text; YYYY-MM-DD in frontmatter and code
- Numbers: Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above
- Percentages: Always use % symbol (not "percent") — "2.65%" not "2.65 percent"
- Currency: €1,200 (not EUR 1,200 or 1200 EUR)
- Ranges: Use en dash — €1,000–€1,500 (not hyphen or "to")

---

## Spelling and Language

We write in British English (not American).

| British | American |
|---|---|
| Organisation | Organization |
| Recognised | Recognized |
| Colour | Color |
| Adviser | Advisor |
| Licence (noun) | License |

Exception: We use "advisor" throughout (not "adviser") as a brand decision.

---

## Acronyms and Abbreviations

First use: Write the full name followed by the abbreviation in parentheses.
Subsequent uses: Use the abbreviation.

Example: "The Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD) processes applications..."

Standard abbreviations for Cyprus processes (always defined on first use):
- CRMD — Civil Registry and Migration Department
- MEU1 / MEU3 — EU national registration forms
- TIC — Tax Identification Code
- GESY — General Healthcare System (Γενικό Σύστημα Υγείας)
- Non-Dom — Non-Domiciled tax status
- HNI — High Net Worth Individual

---

## Links

- Descriptive anchor text only. "Click here" and "read more" are not acceptable.
- Link to the most specific relevant page, not a homepage.
- External links to government sites: open in new tab. Internal links: same tab.
- Never link to a page that does not yet exist (broken UX and SEO).

---

## Accuracy and Review

- Every factual claim must be traceable to a process doc or KB article before publication.
- Published dates and "last updated" dates must be accurate.
- If the underlying process doc is updated, a content review must follow within 2 weeks.
- Do not edit live content to add speculation — note the uncertainty and research before updating.
