### How the buying process works: six stages
~550 words — numbered structure, one H3 per stage:

#### Stage 1: Reservation agreement
2–7 days. Offer accepted; reservation agreement signed; deposit of 3–5% paid. Reservation period (30–60 days) allows for due diligence. Key point: do not sign anything before appointing an independent lawyer.

#### Stage 2: Pre-contract due diligence
2–3 weeks. The buyer's independent lawyer checks: title deed (no encumbrances); plot boundaries vs physical property; building permits; building completion certificate; utility connections; outstanding taxes or charges; environmental restrictions; zoning. This is where problems are caught — do not rush it.

#### Stage 3: Contract of sale
Lawyer drafts the sale and purchase agreement. Must include: full property description and plot number; purchase price and payment schedule; completion date; remedies and penalties; seller's obligation to provide clear title; contingency clause for title deed receipt.

#### Stage 4: Payment schedule
First tranche on signing (10–20%); further staged payments; balance on completion. Key instruction: include a contingency holding back 10–20% until title deed is received.

#### Stage 5: Completion and handover
Final payment made; seller provides discharge confirmations for any mortgages; Deed of Sale signed; property handed over. The buyer becomes the legal owner at this point, even if the title deed has not yet been issued.

#### Stage 6: Title deed application
Buyer's lawyer applies to the Land Registry. This is when the wait begins — covered in detail in the title deed section below.

### The title deed problem — what every buyer needs to know
~400 words — this is the section that will either reassure or alarm; write it with full accuracy without catastrophising. Root causes of the delay: Land Registry backlog; building violations requiring regularisation; developer delays; competing claims; inadequate digitalisation of records. Risks for buyers without a title deed: cannot secure a mortgage; cannot sell easily; no formal legal proof of ownership; inheritance complications. Four protection strategies: (1) contingency clause holding back 10–20% until title deed received; (2) staggered payments linked to completion AND title deed; (3) lawyer assessment of likely delay based on land type and history; (4) Deed of Sale executed promptly on completion. ### Why you must use an independent lawyer
~300 words — practical and direct. What an independent lawyer does: verifies title; checks for encumbrances; reviews building permits and completion certificate; conducts forfeiture searches (outstanding taxes, court judgments, bank charges on title); verifies utility connections are legal; drafts or reviews the contract; manages escrow; handles Land Registry application; manages ministerial approval if required. Conflict of interest explanation: the developer's lawyer acts for the developer — not for the buyer. Specific consequence: no independent due diligence, no contingency clauses protecting the buyer, title issues may be downplayed or missed entirely. Include one realistic scenario: "We've seen buyers sign contracts drawn up by the developer's lawyer with no contingency clause — and spend years waiting for a title deed they could have protected themselves against." Counter the common objection: "The developer said their lawyer handles everything" — explain plainly why this is not in the buyer's interest.
